Isn't that the nature of trading? Price can always go the other way For every reason TO enter a trade there are reasons NOT to enter a trade. There is nothing in the world that I have found that can accurately predict the direction OR the distance a trade will go. This strategy is simply trading the break of the average high/low swings as determined by the Hull MA. We have no idea if the move will continue in our favour or for how long or simply bounce off that level and reverse Nothing tipped me about this trade...I simply followed my rules. I had a pending sell order placed just below the 1.28500 price where the latest Hull swing was (see yellow arrow pointing to the area). Pretty weak swing, I'll admit, but I'm eager I could have used the two earlier swings but I think I prefer to use the most recent. I certainly placed this particular buy stop on the early side of things as the Hull was red and moving down which I assumed was a new swing point but as soon as 8:00 AM hit, up shot the price! If price had gone down I would have caught the move with my sell stop order and I would have placed a buy stop above that higher swing in case price decided to bounce off the 1.28500 level and head for the 1.29000 level. If you look through the charts on the 5 minute GBPUSD you will often find nice moves at the NY open that break the most recent Hull swings in the London session. Those moves easily reach the nearest 25 pip or 50 pip round number price which is where I take profit as that just makes sense to me a as reasonable target that price will attempt to hit before deciding what it wants to do next. I had my TP set a little above the 25 pip round number simply because it had been breached earlier so I figured it would likely get breached again so I set my TP at 15 pips instead of the yellow dotted line (25 pip round number level) like I usually would.

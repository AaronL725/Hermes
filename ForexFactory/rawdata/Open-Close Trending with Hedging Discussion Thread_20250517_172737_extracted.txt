Hopefully, I can explain this thoroughly. Ok here we go. The basic strategy for H1 tKimble is as follows: 1. Open a position north if +50 level is crossed (+50 is 50 pips above the 25LWMA) 2. Open a position south if -50 level is crossed 3. If you have a north bound position and your price crosses the -50 level close the north position and open a south position. 4. If you have a south bound position and your price crosses the +50 level close your south position and open a north position. * Basic adjustment rules: 1. Move your SL with the moving trend. If you have a north bound position and it is still moving north adjust your SL to -50 level on an hourly basis in the north bound direction only. 2. The opposite of #1 is also true for south bound positions. * The basic system does not allow for wipsaws (ranging markets) in a given period. The maximum loss per trade is 100 pips. * The basic system is setup on Stop Losses and your positions close when the reach either the +/-50 level with a range from -100 pips to the current record for this year 896 pips. Hedging Adjustment to this System are as follows: Rule #1 is always never loose pips. Rule #2 if we are ever in doubt and you want to loose pips refer to rule #1. * To achieve rule #1 we will run with out stop losses until our position is positive. Hypothetical example: You open a position at 0:00 gmt on 4-1-07 with 1 lot @ 230.00 when the price hits the +50 level (without SL's). The trend is going north until 4-2-07 @ 3:00 when it goes through the -50 level. Between 4-1 and 4-2 we are monitoring the trend, once the moving up -50 level reaches our price @ 230.00 we set a SL. From there we continue to adjust it until the trend ceases to go north and goes through -50 level on 4-2-07. ** The above is real simple with a trend going in the same direction. The trouble comes when the market is not trending but ranging before we have a chance to adjust (or in this case add a SL) the SL to a 0 or + position. We don't want a margin call and we don't want to loose profits. Hypothetical example #2: In this example we opened a north bound position on 4-1 @ 230.00, this time the pair went south through the -50 level before we could place a positive SL in place. Rule #1 says to never loose pips so we take out a south bound position in an equal or greater amount than our north position. * It would look like this: North position (lot size 1.00) on 4-1-07 233.00 @ 0:00 gmt (+50 level). South position (lot size 1.00 or higher) on 4-1-07 232.00 @ 5:00 gmt. Our south position is our hedge position and protects us from our 100 pip loss. * After you have taken the above position you would have 2 positions open. Depending on which direction the trend went from here you would adjust and add SL according to the basic rules of always placing SL's when in positive pips. (see above) If the trend went south far enough to put your south position in positive territory you would close your south position after the trend came back through the +50 level. and the process continues. ** Note it is possible to have multiple hedges and or positions in place if the market is in a tight ranging motion. Hope I explained that thoroughly enough. If I didn't please let me know and I'll try to re-explain an area that may have been unclear. hope you find the pips your looking for. yezbick

As scientist said you can have a no swap account. I don't know if other brokers offer this. IBFXs' swap rate information is listed below regarding this. IBFX-Swap rate info. http://www.interbankfx.com/swap_rates.php Swap Rates What is SWAP? Swap is a debit or credit paid or earned as a reflection of the varying interest rates applicable to currency pairs. When trading the USD/JPY for example, swap interest rates will be determined based on the interest rates of the countries being represented by this pair. Depending on whether you are long or short and which country has higher interest rates, you may be charged or credited interest. Essentially, when a trader holds a position over night they are subject to the interest rates applicable to the currency pair they are trading. 'Swap' is also commonly referred to as 'rollover rates'. When are swap rates calculated? Swap rates are calculated daily at 4:59 EST. Trades that have been opened before 4:59 EST and held open past this time will be subject to swap rates. Swap rates are tripled on Wednesday at 4:59 EST. Why are swap rates tripled on Wednesday? When placing a trade in the spot Forex market, the actual value date is two days forward. A deal done on Thursday is for value Monday. A deal done on Friday is for value Tuesday, and so on. On Wednesday the amount of swap is tripled in order to compensate for the following weekend (during which time swap is not charged because trading is stopped for the weekend). Can I trade a no swap account? Interbank FX does offer 'no swap' or interest free accounts. However, these accounts are reserved for Muslim clients who are requesting such due to religious beliefs. Clients trading no swap accounts will be charged an additional half pip per round turn trade. If you would like to request a no swap account for religious reasons please note this under the comments section of your account application. The accounts department will then email you a form that will need to be signed and returned.

Recent findings This system has one flaw that will need to be corrected in order for it to become more stable over the long haul. The flaw is that when you hold a hedged position and release the hedge due to another direction change. If the direction change comes to quickly, and you don't recover your existing loss, you could possibly be opening up yourself to an increase loss on the current hedge position. Example: * Position short taken @ 237.00 and hedged at 238.00 with a long position. (to stop the potential loss) * Long position closed @ 239.00 in profit, but movement continues north resulting in a potential additional 100 pip loss to your previous hedged position. ** First short could now be 200 pips down and your account could be 200 pips down in equity. Balance with this system will grow, but your equity will be reduced every time the change of direction is to short to close your previous position in the positive. I think tKimble might be on to something with hedging only inside the +/- 50 levels. This would have the oposite effect where balance might decrease, but equity would be increasing. The H4 time frame does have its rewards as well. Ultimately when you are using the 25 LWMA in the H4 you are using the equivalent of a 100 LWMA itn the H1 timeframe. Just some thoughts.

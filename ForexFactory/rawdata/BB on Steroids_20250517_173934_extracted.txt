I've followed your previous threads for a bit and have also looked at your BB on steroids. It seems to be though that you're just using classic BB methodology. White trades being Bollinger Bounces, and Blue trades being Bollinger Breakouts. In my example, I'm using two indicators that Bollinger himself developed. One if them is the Bollinger %B, and the other is Bollinger Bandwidth. Bollinger bandwidth measures volatility based on the width of the upper and lower bands as you may have guessed. It is thought that the bands contract during periods of low volatility. %B is an overbought/oversold indicator where the close is plotted as a percentage against the bands. This can be used to help indicate a bounce or breakout situation. Now for the explaination of the hollow arrows not on the main chart. GREEN: 1st: Taken long because it goes back into the channel. 2nd: No blue trade here because of positive slope of bandwidth 3rd: White trade. Might have lost depending on your MM. 4th: White trade. 5th-7th: No trade, positive bandwidth slope. 8th: Blue Trade. negative bandwidth slope. RED: 1st: White trade. previous breakout failed. positive slope 2nd: Blue Trade, -slope 3rd: no trade 4th-6th: White trade, again... MM Note: the red vertical lines indicate where you should look. At the second red arrow, and the last green arrow, the slope taken into consideration is from the point of breakout, to the last drastic change in the curve. I wrote this in haste, so forgive me for some errors it may contain. http://img144.imageshack.us/img144/2...pr07oy7.th.gif *it seems that FF might have disabled uploading... *the arrows in main chart are engulfing candlestick patterns. *Gray arrows are fractals. Hey Pipette, I think you may be muddling the two signals together too much when they are in fact very seperate. Blue signals require you to solely focus on the blue bollinger band. If the price closes to the outside of the channel, you have a blue signal. The same goes with white signals. If the price closes inside the channel after having touched a white bollinger band, then you have a white signal. It's important to note that in each case we are talking about a seperate channel; one for blue and one for white. While these are both seperate signals, the only time it should get confusing is when we get both a blue signal AND a white signal. And for that we will let our stochastics tell us what's up. Ch33z3, I hope you are joking, cause I got the indicator from God knows where... I've been to so many sites. I certainly didn't steal it. I just made the small alteration that allows you to input an offset. TSF is an indicator the starts with the regular linear regression series and then adds the slope of the linear regression to each value in the series. So, as I understand it, if you had a 3 day TSF, that would be day linreg of the first period plus the linreg slope, plus the linreg of the second period plus the linreg slope. Then after getting everything added you end up dividing by the number of periods. If you understand the math notation you can look it up on Wikipedia. Linear regression (linreg) is okay, but I have a feeling it reacts to market movement a little faster than I want. I first saw TSF as an indicator in the charting package available from GTFX, but now I'm in love with MT4, I just can't find the indicator anywhere. So I use linreg for now, but I think TSF is probably better as it will accellerate faster when the market jumps ahead. With TSF in GTFX I found I could use an offset of 4 days on daily charts of pairs and it very clearly showed trends ending with very few premature signals. Linreg on the otherhand gives a similar result with an offset of 2, and more premature signals. What is a premature signal? In earlier parts of a move or less quickly sloping markets the linreg is unreliable altogether. In the endgame, the end is not as spectacularly obvious. So TSF starts to be valid sooner in a move (for my purposes) and clearer at the end of the move. What ends up happening is that this indicator accellerates with a trending market, but at say a 20 period, it doesn't adjust very quickly for a change in market direction. If you offset this indicator 2 or more days, you get a cushion for short term pullbacks, but 80% of the time, when the line gets crossed the move is over. You can play with the period and the offset to see what seems to fit best. It works well on some shorter timeframes as well, but the strength of it comes at calling the end, at least temporarily, for long term moves. Cheers, Qman

hi Let me take a stab at it. 1. pg 4. these are valid blue signals because they closed outside the blue channel and outside the white channel, thus they are valid sells. had they closed within the blue and white BBs then they would have been white sells. So for a blue signal it must close outside of blue, unless by closing between the blue and white and having touched the white BB, in which case then it be a white signal, which overrides blue signals. 2. pg 3. the 2 red signals are valid white signals because they both touched the white BB then closed within the white channel. it doesn't matter about candle color 3. pg 3, third red candle is a valid white signal to sell because price came through the upper white BB and closed within the white channel. color of candle does not come into play. So the best way to understand this method initially is to have 2 charts, one with the white BBs and one with the blue BBs. I got confused between the 2 and this was the easiest way to understand since one has nothing to do with the other. The only time that it gets a little confusing is when the candle touches the white BB but closes outside the blue BB, so you get a white and a blue signal, but again, White supersedes blue.

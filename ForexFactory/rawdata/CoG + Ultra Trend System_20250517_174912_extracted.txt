The trading logic is as follow: I normally look for trades in the direction of the main "CoG Moving Average" which is a 240 period (period of the CoG) exponential moving average. This allows for a high probability trading opportunity when the Ultra Trend crosses. So blue line crosses down, with price underneath the CoG "trend" for a sell; and blue line crosses up, with price above the CoG "trend" for a long position. You can however take positions that are irrespective of the CoG trend, and trade in the direction of the cross. I have run into trouble with this, as it is likely that the Ultra trend will re-cross shortly thereafter in the opposite direction. The CoG is useful for filtering out the bad crosses. There are in fact many opportunities where a cross that is not in tandem with the CoG MA is profitable. Looking where price is in the CoG channel displays when such an event occurs. Contrary to CoG MA buy when: price is at least below solid black equilibrium line, or lower into green and red bands/zones. Sell opportunities the opposite. I've been using this system on the 15 min graphs, but it can be expanded out to longer time-frames with similar results. I really want to try and at least develop a method of alert for the Ultra Trend crosses, and maybe fully automate the system with someone's assistance. Then if it meets this clause, I hold on until it reaches the "22" line (for longs) or the "8" line (for shorts)..if the line jumps down and bounces above this line, I liquidate the position, if not I hold on until it hits the "30" line (for longs) or the "2" line (for shorts), rebreaching this line also liquidates the position. You can also play the crossing of certain levels, like when the line breaches the 8 line, heading up for a buy.

No problem. This scheme has changed alot since I first posted it, as it's become much less dependent on the CoG for taking trades. Essentially, we look for confluence in the Slope_Direction_Line indicator and the UT_FAST. We see a high UT_FAST value, we are looking for shorts; and vice versa for longs. The crux of this system is the Slope Direction Line (SDL). By default, it has a period of 20, but I use two iterations; one @ (20) and one @ (35). Trading on the 1hr period, the (35) provides a very clear picture of the daily trend, with the (20) acting as an inter-daily trend indicator. It normally takes several days for the UT_FAST to go from one extreme to another, which dictates that a trade might potentially be open for several days. The MACD I use for confirmations. I normally look to it after I have identified a possible trend change via the SDL and UT_FAST. A cross is the best confirmation you can get, unless it gets choppy along the zero line; and in this case I would either stick with the trade held previous or have it be a no-trade. I have integrated several features of the THV system into this method, including the "Multi-info" and the pivot system. The pivots are excellent indicators for long/short confirmations. Successful shorts are normally taken at Resistance levels, and longs at the Supports. Most of the action is going to take place between R1 and S1, but some of the higher volume pairs regularly exceed the R2 and S2 levels. The CoG is used as a type of barometer for SDL changes. If, for example, PA has touched or exceed the top green level, and a short opportunity appears, it most likely will be a good trade. If we see a SDL change with PA hovering close to the black median level, it will be a poor performer on average. D/L the indicators and play with it. Get a feel for anticipating trend changes (SDL changes) and then looking at the other indicators for guidance on if it will be a good trade. Ride the white and yellow SDLs for a couple of days, as on average they will go for 2-3 days before a legitimate change. As for SLs and TPs, this is up to you. I look to the THV's "3x ATR(20)" as a good source. This is normally about 70% of the daily average (pips) and should price in any wip-saw movement. When you first take a trade, watch it manually for a few hours though. It will become very clear within 3-4 hours after opening the trade whether your interpretations of the signals was genuine. Firstly: Pair's Spread (PS) * 2.5 = y y = Magnitude to set renko block (RB) in RenkoLiveChart indicator Then: Daily Average (DA) _______________ = z y If Z &lt; 10 , don't trade this system with the pair; as there are other, lower spread pairs out there with higher daily averages (aggregate price volume) SUCH AS EURUSD . Theorectically, if you find the spread to be negligible in trading, since intra-day aggregate price volume far out shadows the relatively high spread, it is capitally efficient to trade this pair over of a smaller spread, but lower volume pair. I may have posted a simpler version of this, but here is a slightly more complicated version of the stop-loss and trailing stop-loss method for this system: Basically, a successful SDL delta will yield more than 40 gross pips. However, as I have already demonstrated, we are anticipating HMA deltas to the new SDL. These contrarian HMA deltas are only significant when they trigger the trailing stop loss. The reason we would want to be automatically stopped out is two-fold. Provide a new lower/higher price to re-enter the main SDL trend, once the HMA delta corresponds with the SDL slope again. This is something we want because overall throughout the entirety of the SDL we'll make more aggregate pips. There is also the chance the HMA delta will pierce the SDL, creating a new SDL delta. This event of course dictates a new trend direction, and therefore a contrary trade. (e.g. your long gets stopped out by the trailing-sl, the SDL then changes, you enter a new short w/o losing any more profit than the trailing-sl captured and you're in a strong position for the new trend.) Given, there will be instances where there is minor SDL chop, and you could lose pips, but overall with this system you'll be much more profitable if you follow the above two steps. PA inherently is always trending or changing into a new trend. The chop is indicative of a trend pause or an imminent delta --&gt; so we are always anticipating the trend, (trend is your friend and all that good stuff) .

Money Management. 1. Maximum risk per trade of 1% - 2% of account balance (Depending on Risk Appetite). 2. Profit target per trade of 1% - 2% of account balance (Equal to max risk per trade). 3. Minimum capitalization of $5000. 4. Use a broker that allows micro lots (min 0.01). 5. Max peak to trough drawdown of 16% (17 full losing trades in a row or 25 average losing trades in a row ). Long Entry Signal. 1. 21 EMA crosses above 89 SMA (21 EMA &gt; 89SMA) indicating a change from a bearish to a bullish trend. This signals a “pending long trade” 2. Price climbs and then retraces (usually back to the 21 or 89) with MACD turning red. 3. Enter on 1st closed 4H candle after MACD turns back to green. 4. Long Entry Rules: a. Price of the closed 4H entry candle must be greater than the 21 EMA. If not, wait for next MACD long entry signal (price climbs, retraces changing MACD from green to red and then climbs again changing MACD to green thus signaling a long entry). b. 21 EMA must be greater than 89 SMA. c. Only one trade per 21/89 cross. 5. Long entry Stop-Loss is placed 4-10 pips (depending on pair) below the low of the previous substantial move (best found by using a daily chart). Attached is a graph showing what I mean by “substantial move.” This stop-loss is generally between 200-400 pips depending on the pair. 6. Long entry Take-Profit is placed at a distance equal to the number of pips as the stop-loss (example: 200 pip SL = 200 pip TP). Once TP has been triggered, no new entry until 21/89 short cross. Short Entry Signal. 1. 21 EMA crosses below 89 SMA (21 EMA &lt; 89SMA) indicating a change from a bullish to a bearish trend. This signals a “pending short trade” 2. Price drops and then retraces (usually back to the 21 or 89) with MACD turning green. 3. Enter on 1st closed 4H candle after MACD turns back to red. 4. Short Entry Rules: a. Price of the closed 4H entry candle must be less than the 21 EMA. If not, wait for next MACD short entry signal (price drops, retraces changing MACD from red to green and then drops again changing MACD to red thus signaling a short entry). b. 21 EMA must be less than 89 SMA. c. Only one trade per 21/89 cross. 5. Short entry Stop-Loss is placed 4-10 pips (depending on pair) above the high of the previous substantial move (best found by using a daily chart). Attached is a graph showing what I mean by “substantial move.” This stop-loss is generally between 200-400 pips depending on the pair. 6. Short entry Take-Profit is placed at a distance equal to the number of pips as the stop-loss (example: 200 pip SL = 200 pip TP). Once TP has been triggered, no new entry until 21/89 long cross. Close Position. In the event of a 21/89 cross prior to taking profit or getting stopped out, close the position on the close of the candle where the cross occurs. 2 out of 3 losing trades are closed out this way rather than hitting the SL which is the reason that the average winning trade is 1.34 times the average losing trade. --- 帖子 #39 ---
The magic question that I struggled with as the plan developed was, how do I get the most out of every move? Putting the TP equal to the SL distance results in leaving a lot of pips on the table in the case of the strong, sustained trends. However, what I also found is that those types of trends are rare and so widening my TP, adding to my position, or trailing the stop actually lessened the profitability of the system. Interestingly enough, I started with the tp being equal to the sl but then decided to see if it were worthwhile to increase the tp. To do this, I measured every trend over the last 5 years across 12 pairs and input that data into a complex spreadsheet. I created a "tp multiplier" that allowed me to then test unlimited tp distances. With a few exemptions (eurusd and gbpusd), the tp = sl outperformed any other setup. Keep in mind also, that my primary objective is to make my trading as non-time consuming as possible and so entering, setting a tp/sl, and forgetting about the trade until the next 21/89 really serves me well. However, for those of you with more time to commit to your charts, there is undoubtable a few wrinkles that can be added that would increase your profitablility.

The cross is just a signal to begin looking for a trade in the direction of the cross. We then use an MACD signal to enter in that new direction. So in the case of this GBPJPY trade the cross occurred at 132.24 (132.27 with my broker). That signaled a "pending" trade meaning we are now on alert to enter short once the following happens: 2. Price drops and then retraces (usually back to the 21 or 89) with MACD turning green. 3. Enter on 1st closed 4H candle after MACD turns back to red. 4. Short Entry Rules: a. Price of the closed 4H entry candle must be less than the 21 EMA. If not, wait for next MACD short entry signal (price drops, retraces changing MACD from red to green and then drops again changing MACD to red thus signaling a short entry). b. 21 EMA must be less than 89 SMA. c. Only one trade per 21/89 cross. 5. Short entry Stop-Loss is placed 4-10 pips (depending on pair) above the high of the previous substantial move (best found by using a daily chart). Attached is a graph showing what I mean by “substantial move.” This stop-loss is generally between 200-400 pips depending on the pair. 6. Short entry Take-Profit is placed at a distance equal to the number of pips as the stop-loss (example: 200 pip SL = 200 pip TP). Once TP has been triggered, no new entry until 21/89 long cross. In the case of the GBPJPY trade from post #70, step 2 has ALREADY occurred and now we are only waiting for MACD to turn back red and that candle to close. FYI. The text is the signal option defaulted in your MACD indicator settings. They are annoying and I understand completely what your problem is. Here is what I did: Go into the MACD indicator settings and change 1) Alert on to 'None', 2) Max_alerts to '0', and 3) ShowSignal to 'False'. You will then need to delete all the text objects that the indicator had previously drawn. To do this, go to Charts&gt;Objects&gt;Objects List. Under the objects list delete all text drawn by the MACD custom indicator. I then save my template as "default" which saves your MACD settings and makes it so you don't ever have the problem again when you open a new chart.

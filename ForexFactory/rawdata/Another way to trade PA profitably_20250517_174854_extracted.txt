I started using COT way back but did not really understand what it meant until I read Larry Williams. He's an expert in COT reports. Anyway, combining price action with COT has been really profitable for me. These trades generally last longer, generate a lot of pips (usually I get 500+ pips) and I only look at daily, weekly and monthly charts. Basically you want to pay attention when both the commercials and small speculators are in extreme and opposite positions. I leave out the large speculators because generally they're trend followers. We want to get into a trend right when it starts and get out when it ends. Open interest is optional but could be used to determine the strength of the trend. If the commercials are extremely short and take 70% of all positions you know a major trend reversal is coming so watch for reversal price action patterns. Make it simpler, COTs are used for picking market tops/bottoms, price action for entry. I use a really wide stop loss, usually 200 pips or last swing low, sometimes I don't use stop loss at all if I'm really, really confident. This is really difficult to explain in words so I'm going to put up some charts so you guys can better understand how this works. The commercials DO NOT make money by trading, my friend. So even if they're long along the bearish trend, they do NOT lose, NOR break-even! They still profit, just not from trading. Japanese customer has just received a large order and they need the money now. So, the commercial bank does the analysis and finds out that the Japanese customer can not get the loan because yen is NOT going to appreciate. So, this bank decides to turn away the application. The loan still works and produces money. If yen starts to depreciate, the futures will offset conversion loss in a foreign exchange transaction. If this bank is even smarter (and I am sure they will be) they will open a line of credit with several tranches to secure themselves from the risks of non-delivery and market conditions at that time.

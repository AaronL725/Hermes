I'm short the GBPUSD, and long the USDCAD, stupid I know, but I'm demoing right now trying to figure out the meaning of life, etc etc etc. Here's what I've found, and it is rather significant I feel. Trendline breaks. I have been looking at them. There are different types of break. For now, lets just categorize them into two families. The first, and in my opinion, more unreliable break, is the horizontal break. In this scenario, price almost gets 'pushed' over the trendline, but is either moving in a horizontal direction, or is moving in a direction opposite to the break. Perfect example, the USDCAD break, this morning. This trade is -24 pips right now. Notice how price was trending up in the 12 hours preceeding the break, and there is no conviction in the move across the trendline: http://i12.photobucket.com/albums/a2.../aausdcad1.gif The second family of trendline breaks are always welcome. They are vertical breaks. This is where price, like a knife, pierces the trendline in a manner which is convincing. Usually the break candle is a continuation of a previous move, though not always. What is important is that instead of simply being pushed over the trendline, price appears to crack it with certainty and resolve. Picture, words, thousand, here it is, the GBPUSD break today, right now up 40 pips: http://i12.photobucket.com/albums/a2.../aagbpusd1.gif Thoughts, as always, are welcome.

To accurately ID a trend one must look at the next higher TF. I have never seen a trend measurement work well on the TF used for entries (in this case the 4 hr. TF). My personal rule; trends are ALWAYS defined on the next higher TF. This is not to say that trends don't form on the entry TF. But typically there will be several machinations as the trend on the daily TF gradually yeilds to the new trend on the 4 hr. TF. For convenience, and since Mouteki's strat is oriented around the 4hr TF, I use a daily chart to ID trends. Additionally, of all TF's used by traders, daily TF's are no doubt one of the most common. The "next higher" TF always helps to put things into a context. Just as the ripple on the ocean is in the context of the wave, the wave in the context of the tide, and the tide in the context of a tsunami, looking to the next higher TF is a must when establishing the probability of a long or short position being successful. Going with the bias of the next higher TF will always increase your chances of success as well as your potential R/R ratio. If looking for ways make your Mouteki trades HP (High Probability), it would be beneficial to first ID the bias of the daily charts using whatever trend analysis you prefer (seems every trader prefers a different method of trend analysis in my experience). Once this bias is established, look for DS extremes on your 4 hr chart AGAINST the daily bias. This set up is referred as a "Slingshot" as per the dacharts.com gang and is the core of my trading. Additionally, a fib on the 4 hr. that is signaling in the direction of the daily bias is highly complimentary. If you get both (a countertrend DS pullback @ a fib. - see #2 below), take it was a wink and a nod from the market ;-) I provided a few examples. The first is a daily chart of the EUR/USD. One hugely important and hugely overlooked "filter" (for want of a better word) is.......if you're trading 4H, check your 1D charts when drawing your trendlines or deciding whether you take a trade or not. If the 1D chart is choppy and currently in a range (you could probably see that from 4H too!), you might get a trendline rejection (false signal). If 1D has started a strong trend and your Mouteki signal in 4H is against that 1D trend, you should be careful, you're entering into a potential retracement in 1D and this might fall short of your projected target. If your 1D is trending and has retraced and then you get a signal in 4H in the direction of a trend, you have a potentially very good trade. If 1D is approaching a mouteki line in its own chart, enter and then rethink the situation when it hits that line. There might be a retracement from that line. Or it might breach it. Then you'll consider adding to your position on 1D as well. Often false signals in 4H happen when after a period of trending, price reverses and breaches our Mouteki TD only to go back again to do a double top or double bottom and then reverse once again. That's why the safest Mouteki trades are where we already have a double top or double bottom and the Mouteki signal identifies a breach of it Disproportionately long candles....often caused by news releases or unexpected news (eg, a particularly unpopular president of an unnamed country gets assassinated). This very long candle could breach our trendline and give a signal. We enter. Only to see that this was a very temporary reaction to the news. That's just another thing to be careful with. I myself try to stay clear of extremely long candles. Leave them to news-traders, fundamentalists and scalpers.

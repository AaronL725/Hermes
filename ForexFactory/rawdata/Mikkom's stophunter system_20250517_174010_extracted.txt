There is a very good reason I use the opening and 20 pip tp there, I quote mark fisher a bit http://www.investopedia.com/printabl.../04/032404.asp (ACD system linked is also worth reading as is his whole "logical trader" book) Note the bolded section. As the opening range tends to be the point where the market decides its daily direction, it's also one of the lowest risk exit point for the day - violent moves almost always happen AWAY from the opening range. Why then not just close there, why set the 20 pip tp? I want to exploit the random nature of the markets. The 20 pip is there to squeeze some extra pips out of the market and as opening range tends to be the place where the price tries to decide it's direction, it almost always fluctuates 20 pips to the correct direction during the day. How many times you have seen a candle with open nearer than 20 pips around the high or low? If one of these happens during the day [to the wrong direction] then you have a miss on tp but this is very rare. edit: Just to clarify, opening range of london session could very well be very good point as well but it doesn't fit my system, someone else might want to use it.

Hi Dezil, There were times when stop orders were not originally placed because there was no opposite fractal. However, when the opposite fractal had formed, I could then determine position size and place any stop orders that were missed out initially. This returned more trades on the reversal of a trend or retracement of a large trend (obviously) but had the same expectancy (no better or worse than a standard trade setup.) It's the greater number of trades overall which had a positive outcome that leads me to the conclusion that as long as an opposite fractal had formed and position size can be determined, it is worth doing. However, the same cannot be said for using a non-opposite fractal (e.g. using the distance between a lower fractal and another lower fractal when the trend is bullish and visa versa in a bearish trend). This returned a lower positive expectancy, I think because the distance between fractals was too small on occasion and was therefore more prone to whipsaws.

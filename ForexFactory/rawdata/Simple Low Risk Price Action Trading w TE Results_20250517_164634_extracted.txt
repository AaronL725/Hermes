This is a strategy that I have been developing for some time now and has proven to be very consistent with great risk/reward opportunities if done properly. This strategy works best during London and US open but can be used during the Aussie session and Tokyo with smaller profit targets and stop losses. I use mainly the 15 minute chart and the 1 hour chart is used to double check trades or to take a longer term trade. Take profit and stop loss will vary upon the trade but do not take a trade unless you have at least a 1:1 risk/reward. I typically take a 1:2 risk/reward or higher. With all 4 signals lining up you can be more aggressive with the target profit. I will keep my trade explorer visible as I continue to test this strategy. Any constructive input is appreciated. You will need a platform that has these indicators and these settings: Parabolic SAR (Red Dots) Stochastic Full w/ 70 High 30 Low 5 K Period 3 D Period (Red/Blue lines at bottom of screen) 100 EMA (Orange Line) Bollinger Bands (Blue lines on chart) As far as the order of significance to the strategy, I look for the signals in this order: 100 EMA Stochastic Full Parabolic SAR Bollinger Bands The idea is to have at least 3/4 indicators have correct signals to open the trade. This should happen a few times during the day depending upon how many pairs you are watching. Trades with 4/4 indicators will always have the best risk/reward. --- 帖子 #20 ---
You can certainly trade it that way. I recommend loading up the indicators or template and finding where the signals line up and see if you would benefit from entering once the stoch reaches above 30 or below 70. What is going to happen just like any system is that if you wait to enter, you may have a higher win rate, but your risk/reward will suffer because you will have to increase your stop loss and decrease your target profit due to the market already moving. This may differ for each trader and their comfort level but I would rather take the trade at the absolute best risk/reward than to wait for the market to make the move. What that typically means is that if I see all 4 signals (or a great setup with 3) I'll try to get as close to the PSAR or bollinger band as I can while the stoch is close to 30 or 70 or exceeding. If I can get at least a 1:2 risk reward on each trade I don't have to be right even 50% of the time to be profitable. Luckily this system should yield much higher results than that if done properly so there is room for entries to not be perfect. The only thing strict about the system is to at least have 3 signals lining up and to not take a trade under 1:1 risk/reward. As you get used to the system you'll be able to make decisions on your entries and exits based upon previous experience.

So divergence is an extremely important thing to be aware of and look for in any strategy, it's just a common tool in any form of trading. Here's how to identify it with stochastic: First, AngelicaMari just shared a good indicator for stochastic divergence and I recommend downloading it if you want help identifying when it happens. It's actually very common and paired with other indicator signals it can make great moves. In this picture each orange line on the stochastic is bullish divergence meaning the previous low point was higher than the current low point and both under the 30 stoch line. The divergence happens when on the chart you see that the previous low point is lower than the current low point. The chart makes more sense than that explanation. Bearish divergence is the opposite of course. You look for the previous high to be lower than the current high resulting in that divergence line slanting upwards and the price. Second picture is how you could essentially weave in and out of trades using just divergence and the 100 EMA. I wouldn't take all of these trades but on days where you don't get great setups it's very useful to know what else to look for.

Here's an example of a great money management strategy for this system which can also go along with any other system really but it works well for making great entries and exits. Basically the idea is to half your normal position and scale into a trade instead of the single order trades. This allows you two opportunities at the perfect entry to ensure the best price. I have a couple examples of this, one being the EURGBP trade from yesterday that I saw forming just around close of the market. In the EURGBP trade the original signal was breached but did fall into a support level and bounced off it about 10 pips. So if you had set your original entry in that range you would be up 10 pips, however it falls back through that support to the next support and then skyrockets off that support. If you had set your SL just below the orignal support you would have stopped out. But if you had noticed the two supports and placed a buy order around each support with a SL below the second support, you would scale into the position very well and the likelihood of the trade going your way increases. Worst case scenario is that the trade keeps falling through the second support but the SL was so minimal that you really don't take much extra damage. So the risk/reward is good on the first entry, the second risk/reward is GREAT. On this NZD/USD trade I took on one of my demos (as I'm testing scaling) I sort of scaled it backwards. I am showing the 15 minute chart but was mostly looking at the 1 Hour chart for this trade. I took the first entry as I saw the setup forming and placed an entry a couple pips below the 100 EMA with a target profit at the lowest support. The price moved to the previous higher low and bounced off it. If I wasn't scaling this move I probably would have cut the trade at this support but knowing I still have the other half of the trade to enter, I waited for the pullback off this support and entered once it reached resistance. So once the price moved to resistance and there was another bearish divergence, the price moved all the way down to the low support to hit both TPs.

Basically in the movie they would play at the blackjack tables in different casinos, where the spotters were keeping the count, once the count is good they signal a 'big' player to come to the table and play big money (player has the edge). Simple! SO then i started to ask myself how could this idea be used in FX... Forex Market = Casino All my 1HR charts = Blackjack tables BBands and MAs (HiLo) = Spotters ME and my Experience = Big players (money on line) By using the same principles of counting cards, you would have to wait for the deck to get hot before putting your hard earned money on the line. Now the hard part is, keeping emotions out! It must be done, you must be a robot when trading, keeping to the system or else you will second guess every trade and cripple your account. Entries/Exits/Targets : With this trading system, i have been basically trading almost around the clock, taking positions at anytime (but 90% of my trades are taken during 9am-4pm est). The toughest part in trading is not entering the market but exiting. This is where i am very open to other ideas. Counter Trend Trading: When trading this method, you do not always have to keep going with the trend, especially when you start to believe that the 30M chart is heading into major S/R or its just exhausted. The way I countertrend trade is being very patient and waiting for a some sort of signal to show that price is being a bit overbought or oversold. And i do this by waiting for long wicks on the 30M chart showing exhaustion (which means waiting for the candles to close ). After i notice that the rally is having trouble going higher i will then wait for a spike in the 5M before getting into the market using the highest/lowest price as a SL. There must be though, at least 50 pips between the Price and the HiLo MAs, showing a strong rally. For me counter trend trading is bit more riskier than going with trend, so i do not trade a full size and i am looking for price to head back towards the HiLo MAs on the 30M chart as my exit strategy. The main reason to counter trend is when the move happens and you missed it (ex. wake up after london's session and GBP/USD is down 250 pips better risk reward to play pullback). Just remember to trade smaller when taking these trades. For me, a successful trader can switch gears quickly without 'self doubt' or '2nd guessing.' Another thing that i would like to mention here is that you should be able to tell what type of market you are in by your losses, if you take 1 or 2 losses with the trend, switch gears! the market is telling you to! just much harder to ACTUALLY DO. Interesting method. Similar to Joe Ross Camel Back method (he uses 40ema tunnel). I like the use of the two time frames which helps to filter out some of the noise. However, I think your entry would be more enhanced by considering using a Traders Trick entry (google Joe Ross Law of Charts -its free), as a lot of times in a strong trend price will not pull back to the ema tunnel (for multiple entries throughout a trade). Deeper pull backs on the other hand, could be enhanced by placing your entries at the low of the cluster of candles at the swing high (eg. down trend) as this provides resistance much of the time. Be ready with a Traders Trick entry just in case that resistance level isn't hit. If the pull back supercedes this candle this is usually a warning of an iminent change of direction, and your thrust back down from the pull back will likely be weak. Some profits may then be taken at the swing low of the pull back (The hook). Using both entry methods provides an entry insurance with the entry at resistance, the very top of the pull back, being the most lucrative entry. If price does not reach it then the TT entry will get you in. With regards to profit targets this I think is important in understanding each trade's risk reward ( I never use less than a 2:1). Targets might be SR levels calculated against your stop at the BB band (as stated by the op) or a previous swing high/low. Plot SR levels to Make sure you are not selling directly into demand or buying into supply, the BB bands won't tell you this (and to be honest I am not really sure how useful the BB bands are). For targets without obvious SR levels I often use an A-B projection from C approach (previous swing projected from the current pull back) as a first target (similar to fib projections). If I have a confluence of a target projection and an SR level, this makes the target highly probable. Taking some profits at targets and letting the remainder of your trade to trail means you won't be left out if the market makes a runner.

I think you are right to some extent with regards to risk:reward. But it depends how you analyze the charts. I don't think you can suggest how the pro's trade as I am sure they trade a vast array of techniques. No one trader is the same. However, there is one dominant level of understanding pro traders have and that is order flow. This is because they can see it (less so in Forex). The closest technique we have to seeing it on our charts is by understanding supply and demand levels and knowing where these are formed. These can be used to determine targets and stops. They are not 'magic' as you might suggest but they are reliable. The point I wish to make is that such levels including SR, can be used with this method as a guide. Should you have a signal on a 5 min chart to go long directly below a higher time frame supply level, this would be at a far greater risk than if you took a long signal near a higher time frame demand level. Whilst I agree that your method has an edge in terms of feeding off the momentum of a higher time frame on a pull back at a lower time frame, understanding levels of SR or Supply/Demand would put the odds even more in your favour. At the end of the day trading is about reducing risk and maximising reward.

If a trade works out from the begginning, meaning I am never down more than the spread...I will put my SL @ break even once I am up a considerable amount and wait to see where the price action slows down for me to get out, sometimes thats only 25 pips and sometimes its 150 pips. Exiting positions to me is more art than science, just noticing how PA is acting (you'll get there, lots of screen time). But when I enter a position and it keeps going against me and I'm in the red most of the trade, i will just cut the position out when it goes in my favor, even 10-15 pips. That's only bc the pair didn't act the way i thought it would...we are all wrong a lot of time! Take your loss and RUN! Try not to hold on too long to losses! There will be many more set ups to come. What help me the most to learn to cut losses is keeping a detailed trading journal. Hopefully that answers your question. MADNESS

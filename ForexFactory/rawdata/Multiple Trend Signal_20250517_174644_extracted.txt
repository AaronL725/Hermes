If this system works, it can probably be polished. Much depends on the codependence structure of the signals, their correlation in particular. And this correlation changes substantially from one period to another. In the idealized and degenerate case {correlation = 100%}, when we say that we trade using 7 signals we say nothing. Our decision making still depends on only one underlying factor. On the other hand, in the periods when the signals are relatively independent, combining several of them may be a good idea. It may result in substantial alpha enhancement... Another issue here is that some signals may be closer than others. Signals A and B advocating the same trade may not mean as much as signals A and C advocating the same trade... So number 7 seems a bit arbitrary, time-wise and portfolio-wise... Has anybody tried signal aggregation in a bit more systematic and scientific manner? For example, it would not be difficult to estimate the joint distribution of signals using a dynamic copula . Then we could see which region corresponds to the 10% of most extreme cases, 5% of the most extreme cases, and so on. And then we could trade whenever the portfolio of signals fell into that region (which would be time-varying, of course).... This is not high mathematics or a "bubble" of somebody who is into advanced statistics. There is a lot of data here and, with a relatively simple copula family, we would generate stable estimates... There are other approaches as well (based on somewhat more advanced statistical techniques).

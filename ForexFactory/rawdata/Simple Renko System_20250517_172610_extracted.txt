Jim, The data scrunched means you don't have very much history. Download some more history. The Sixths indicator is basically the same as a stochastic Oscillator. It is taking a certain period and calculating if the price is above/below a certain median. Basically, if you are buying with these settings you are saying for the last 50 bars of a range of 50 pips, the screen is divided into sixths (16.666~) etc.. So, when you buy in the gold area, you are in the bottom 16%, much like when/if you would buy with RSI or a straight Stochastic. The reason Bob uses this with his method is that he has shorter timeframe stochs that he follows, along with a visual longer term stoch (Sixths) that will keep him buying in oversold and selling in overbought as a counter-trend trader. The challenge with this 'ES' is that how do you know where your exit is? Break of ATR, hit the middle line, hit the other green line, etc.. MPTM with 50 pips jump etc.. I am building 3-4 EA's right now that are working with SuperTrend &amp; Renko and regular charts. The Renko takes up so much in resources for a clearer screen that it may not be worth it. It sure is a nice look though. If you can take a super-trending currency like EURAUD and just buy on the bars that are green (first one) and sell on the bars that are red (first one) etc.. and Martingale out of them if you get into trouble, then it alone is profitable. When I tried to get my head around Renko candle movement worked in a ranging market, it seemed to me that it took 2 candles to establish a movement. Since then, I have tried 1 candle ago - this works great in a trending market and gets cut to ribbons in a ranging market. Now, anybody with a brain cell should be able to play with my code and tinker the the robot to their hearts content. mql4 has four arrays that deal with a candle's open, close, high and low. These are: Open[] Close[] High[] Low[] Inside the square brackets, we put a digit that refers to a particular candle. '0' means the current candle. Therefore Open[0] will give us the open price of the current candle. From here only requires the slightest overcoming of our schoolday maths teaching. We are taught that that numbers to the left of 0 are negative numbers, -1, -2 etc. When thinking about previous candles, we traders are looking backwards in time but have to drop the negative bit of the digit. Therefore, the open price of the previous candle is accessed by Open[1] not Open[-1]. Two candles ago would be Open[2] and so on. The same applies to Close[], High[] and Low[]. Some examples: Low[5] returns the low of the previous candle no 5 High[3] returns the high of the previous candle no 3 So, play with the robot. Right click the robot select 'Modify' when the source code appears in the editor window. scroll down to the line of code that reads if (Open[0] &gt; Open[2]) change this to if (Open[0] &gt; Open[1]) or to if (Open[0] &gt; High[1]) or to if (Open[0] &gt; Close[1]) or replace the various [1] with [2] or [3] then find the line of code that reads if (Open[0] &lt; Open[1]) and repeat your experiments After each source code modifications, press the F5 key to compile the code you have edited, sit back and watch how the newly-edited bot takes trades and decide for yourself which setting is best.

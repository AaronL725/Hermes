Hi all, just to cut down on the secrecy a bit, I'd like to explain my understanding of the stoch difference indicator/ea. It appears that some people do not understand the indicator they're betting their money on, which is NOT a good idea. Trading is sufficiently difficult by itself, we don't need to turn these forums into IQ tests. Imagine you have A) stochastic with the period 10 on the M30 timeframe and B) stochastic with the period 5 on the H1 timeframe. Those two are "similar", i.e. the two will always be equivalent at the close of the higher TF candle, since the period covered by both stochastic oscillators will be exactly the same. To illustrate: - - - - - - - - - - 10 candles at the lower TF --- --- --- --- --- 5 candles at the higher TF Please note that the two can show a different readout when the higher-TF candle is not about to close. What can happen in this example is that almost 10 lower-TF periods ago there was, say a maximum M and it turns out that the higher TF candle will only close in 30 minutes. M____M' - - - - - - - - - - 10 candles at the lower TF takes M into account so the max is M (&gt;M') __--- --- --- --- - 5 candles at the higher TF does not take M into account so the max is M' (I use underscore _ instead of blanks/spaces, because the formatting doesn't turn out right with spaces) The two stoch oscillators will be different because the A) Stoch(30M, period 10)=(current_price-minimum)/(M-minimum), where, again M is the maximum within the last 10 candles which is time-wise last ~5 h (~10*30). B) Stoch(1H, period 5) =(current_price-minimum)/(M'-minimum), where M' is the maximum within the last 5 candles, which is time-wise last ~4.5 h . The higher maximum M has left the time window at the higher TF. When the higher TF candle closes in 30 minutes, the M will leave also the lower TF window so both maxima will be M', and the stoch values will agree again. Cheers, k

My opinion: If you are observing a stoch difference on a current bar, this method will ensure that the current point is neither a minimum nor a maximum on a period of your stochastic (if it were, which is highly unlikely anyway, both stochastic oscillators would likely agree and be 0/100), so the current market state is most likely not a strong trend. Therefore, prices get revisited with a high probability. As with many similar methods, be careful of expectation of a revisit in case of a strong trend/news event/etc. k

it seems you haven't fully grasped the concept of dissimilarity yet, which is fine. All the indicator is doing, is drawing a horizontal line at the price at which the two different stochastics are showing different values. So naturally it will draw it where price is. The theory is that after this line is drawn, and then price moves away from it in either direction, that price will eventually come back and touch the line again, i.e the line will act as a magnet. One of the main problems is the drawdown, i.e price may come back to the line after 200 pips and you bought after it went 10 pips away then you had to suffer a 190 pip drawdown

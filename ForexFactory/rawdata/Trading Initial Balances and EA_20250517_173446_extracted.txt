What Are Initial Balances? Initial balances refer to a specific time frame at the beginning of a trading session, typically the first few minutes or hours when a market opens. During this period, traders closely observe price movements and volume to gauge market sentiment and establish trading strategies for the rest of the session. How Initial Balance Trading Works The concept of initial balance trading is rooted in the idea that the price and volume behavior during the initial balance period can provide valuable insights into the day's trading potential. Here's how it works: 1. Observation: Traders closely watch the price action and volume during the initial balance period, which often includes the first 30 minutes to one hour of a trading session. This is a critical phase for assessing market dynamics. 2. Key Levels: Traders identify key price levels during the initial balance period, such as the high and low points. These levels can serve as significant reference points for the day's trading activities. 3. Breakouts: Breakouts above or below the initial balance range can signal potential trading opportunities. A breakout above the initial balance high may suggest bullish momentum, while a breakout below it may indicate bearish sentiment. The Expert Advisor So with the EA I've created you trade the initial balance of the currency or index. So say you're trading the NY session. The initial balance would be from 9:30am EST to 10:30am EST. The EA will take the high and low of that time, and if price trades above the high it buys and if price trades below the low it sells. Attached Image (click to enlarge) Trading_Time_Start : What time the EA should start trading. For example if you're trading NY session you want it to trade AFTER the IB is established so for example at 10:31am EST. (You would have to convert to your server time) Trading_Time_End: Time the EA should top trading. Default is 23:55 server time. Initial_Balance_Start: From what time you want the EA to start calculating the IB (convert to your server time and what session you want to trade) Initial_Balance_End: From what time you want the EA to stop calculating the IB (convert to your server time and what session you want to trade) Lot_Size: Pretty straight forward, this is the lot size you want to trade. Take_Profit_Percent: The take profit is by default 2x the size of the SL, you can edit this. SL_IB_Start: The SL is set at the opposite side of the IB. So, if a buy position is opened the SL is set at the low of the IB. If a sell position is opened, the SL is placed a the high of the IB. So this variable is for you to indicate the start of the IB. SL_IB_End: This variable is for you to indicate the end of the IB so it can calculate where to set the SL when a position is opened. Percent_Away: This variable is for the trailing stop. When your position is say 50% or 80% into profit it will send the signal to the trailing stop to start. Percent_For_Trailing_Stop: When your position is say 50% or 80% into profit it will send the signal to the trailing stop to start. This percentage is used as a way to determine how far away you want the SL to trail price.

The markets are dynamic and so setting your stop loss at a fixed number of x pips/points away your entry in every situation might be not a good idea. I haven't tried implementing such a stop myself because it takes a bit of time manually testing it and I don't have the MT4 coding skills myself. My idea would be to try using trailing stop like "1,5 times ATR below the previous candle's low" - for a BUY entry. "1,5 times ATR above the previous candle's high" - for a BUY entry. Regarding to time frames. Price/time is the same on all time frames and you basically trade the breakout of x pre-defined period of time (range) here. Timeframe doesn't really matters. Typical ATR time periods used vary between 5 and 21 days. Wilder originally suggested using 7 days, short-term traders use 5, and longer term traders 21 days. Multiples between 2.5 and 3.5 x ATR are normally applied for trailing stops, with lower multiples more prone to whipsaws. You will probably have to experiment/backtest which are the "best" parameters here.

Here this might give you a few more ideas to improve your EA. For the entry, consider the following ideas/filters: - Wait for a retest of x bar's close after the breakout level (first M5/M15 bar close above/below the high/low of the initial balance) before entering a buy/sell order. The reason for this is, often the price will retest the initial balance highs and lows. This may help to avoid some whipsaws and/or place a tighter stop. E.g. in the case of a break and retest directly above/below the closed retest bar where you place the order instead of the previous initial balance. - Wait for a break/retest of the breakout level with volume, e.g. The bar that breaks or retests the breakout level should have a certain minimum size, if possible no wicks etc. - No entry if price already moved over 50% of the daily ATR(14). The reason for this is, it might not make sense trying to capture a larger part of a potential daily move in one direction if a large price move already happened earlier in the day. This makes particular sense if you are looking to make a bigger profit by the end of the day instead of having a very narrow fixed take profit level. - No buy/sell entry if the initial balance is below/above yesterdays daily high/low. I've never tested this myself, but might also help. The idea here is to only buy if you have buyers behind you and only sell if you have sellers behind you. For the exit: - ATR based trailing stop (like mentioned earlier). The reason for this is, you don't want to get stopped out by "noise" and price fluctuating around. You want to give it some room to actually work out, on the other hand, not too much and, if possible, in line with the prevailing volatility. With the ATR you might able to solve this. You have a tighter stop on calmer days and a wider stop when volatility is high. Since this is a day trading strategy where you want to realize profit at the end of the day and don't want to have any overnight risk, it makes sense to weight the ATR on the first X bars of the day or so rather than those of the past days/weeks. On the other hand, why not let winners run regardless of whether the day is over or not? Most traders seem to do the opposite and drag on losses and cut off profits... - A way to add to your winning trades in some objective/logical/repeatable procedure. From all my experiences, this is a crucial difference between truly successful traders. They have no problem increasing their winners, but keep their losers small with iron discipline - Take profit targets based on Fibonacci levels that are plotted above/below the initial balance. Other: - A parameter to define the maximum trades per day - A parameter to define the minimum/maximum size of the initial balance range high-low (in points) - A parameter to define the max. spread at time of an entry - A parameter to define a entry buffer/offset of x points above/below the initial balance.

Hey everyone, So I managed to at least fix the issue with the levels and times that the EA takes positions. The rectangle, is something I'm still working on. But okay, back to the levels and times the EA takes positions, no need to download a new EA or reinstall anything, it's an issue of setting the times. For starters, the EA works on the server time. So, go to your 30 min chart &gt; identify the candle where the NY session starts ( We're going to be trading U.S. indices on U.S. market times for this example ) &gt; Set that candle as your "Initial_Balance_Start". In my case, on my server it's the 16:30 candle. Now, we have to set the correct "Initial_Balance_End". Remember that the Initial Balance is the first hour of the trading session (9:30am EST to 10:30am EST). So in theory, according to my server time that would be the 16:30 and the 17:00 candle. Previously, the EA had the "Initial_Balance_End" as 17:30. Which is in theory correct, but what the EA was doing was also counting the high and low of the 17:30 candle, when in reality the 17:30 candle is where we want to start trading. So how do we set the correct "Initial_Balance_End"? Well, it's really simple, set it to one minute before the "Trading_Time_Start". So, in my case, if my trading start time is 17:30, I will set my "Initial_Balance_End"as 17:29. What will this do? What does this do? With the above changes what the EA will do is take the high and low of the 16:30 and 17:00 candle, and on candle 17:30 open, it will start trading if price goes above or below the high or low of the 16:30 and 17:00 candle. --- 帖子 #121 ---
What? No! The whole point of the EA is to take trades when there is a break out above the high or below the low of the initial balance. That's the whole idea of trading initial balances. Here's a little more if you need a refresher: https://www.forexfactory.com/thread/...alances-and-ea Again, the TP is the SL multiplied. That being said: There will always be a SL (RISK MANAGEMENT) The SL is always going to be the opposite side of the break out. Example: if price breaks above the IB high and a long position is taken the SL will always be the IB low and if price breaks below the IB low and a short position is taken the SL will always be the IB high. Explanation of TP calculation: TP is the SL multiplied. So again as explained in the image that I sent you, let's set an example where the pips from the position to the SL is 10 pips. If the "Take_Profit_Percent" in the EA is set to 100 then the TP will be 100% of the SL. So in this case: 10 pips (SL) x 1 = 10 pips. If the "Take_Profit_Percent" in the EA is set to 200 then the TP will be 200% of the SL. 10 pips (SL) x 2 = 20 pips. If the "Take_Profit_Percent" in the EA is set to 0 then there will be an error. Why? In math something x zero equals zero. 10 pips (SL) x 0 = 0 pips. This will throw off an error and no position will be taken because the EA will not be able to calculate a TP. The "Take_Profit_Percent" in the EA has nothing to do with where an entry is made, it's only designed for you to calculate your take profit levels. That's why it's labeled as "Take_Profit_Percent". Hope that helps clear things up a little.

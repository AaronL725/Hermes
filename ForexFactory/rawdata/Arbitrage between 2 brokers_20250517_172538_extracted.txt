Interesting thread! I've done something like this before. Not this exactly but a similar concept. If I were to attempt another go at it (and I'm not saying I am planning to), I would do some things differently. I'll get to that in a second. But first I have to ask why both bid and ask prices aren't transferred in a single function call, rather than doing two calls (get_terminal_2_bid/get_terminal_2_ask ) to get these prices when presumably every millisecond counts? If I were to try this again I would add a third price feed and a controlling algorithm for the order in which trades are transmitted. The third price feed would be a fast ECN type feed that has reliable real market prices that I feel represent the actual cash market value (most of the time). The reason is this. Whether you realize it or not, this method relies on the fact that one (or both) of the br0kers has mis-priced its quote. It will usually just be one br0ker. This may be due to latency or some internal br0ker reason for shading prices, but it's usually going to be latency that drives profit. Reasons aside, on any given transaction you're taking advantage of one of the br0ker's prices. When push comes to shove and the br0kers start figuring out what you're doing (which probably won't take long), they will implement countermeasures. This is a virtual certainty. In that case, you will want to ensure that the br0ker's price that you're taking advantage of is executed first. If this trade doesn't execute, there is no reason to execute the second trade that is fairly priced according to the 3rd data feed because you have no edge there. I realize that you're probably seeing some things on demo testing that make your mouth water, but believe me when I say that it won't take long before you're forced to face up to these realities I just posted and to adapt in quick order. A quick primer on arbitrage. After reading through this thread it seems there is a lack of the proper definition of "arbitrage" when applied to forex. Here is how I see it: True arbitrage happens when there are two separate markets offering the same product for different prices. Historically, the Chinese have favored silver, while the Europeans favored gold. A fairly popular method of trading back then would have merchants buying silver in Europe, trading silver for Gold in China, and trading that gold for more silver in Europe. So, numerically: Assume you get 2 silver for 1 gold in Europe, and you get 2 gold for 1 silver in China. I acquire 1000 pieces of silver in Europe. I trade the 1000 pieces of silver for 2000 pieces of gold in China I trade the 2000 pieces of gold for 4000 pieces of silver in Europe. I trade the 4000 pieces of silver for 8000 pieces of gold in China. And the cycle continues until the trader died, was robbed, or otherwise couldn't continue trading. When applying this to the Forex Market, remember that the forex market is made up of several liquidity providers each collaborating together to trade one currency for another. Let's say there is one bank in the US, and one bank in Australia. Both banks are liquidity providers in the Fx market. They therefore receive news at different times. So assume this bomb (true story!) actually went off in Germany, thus crippling the german economy, and further crippling the Euro. The US and the Australian liquidity providers generally find out about this information at different times, and their prices reflect this difference accordingly. This would give me an opportunity to buy some currency from one bank, and sell the same currency to another, all while profiting instantly. Traditional methods of 'arbitrage' in the retail forex world involve the speed at which information travels as opposed to true arbitrage. Retail brokers have to go through a liquidity provider, who themselves have to go through a liquidity provider, before reaching an interbank who ultimately fills an order. This leads to some lag in the true price in the broker's feed, and what also leads to some enterprising individuals who would get a faster currency feed, which can 'predict' a broker's price as much as 30 seconds in advance. The trader would then merely have to watch the faster movement, and apply the trades to the slower feed. This is what brokers do not like. Now, considering the events around the world as of late, arbitrage opportunities are actually more frequently occurring again because of the differences between the speed information travels from the source to the interbanks. The arbitrage opportunities are most obvious at the week's open, when each bank has factored in the weekend's news into their Bid/Ask. Thing is, each bank factors in the news differently, which leads to some instances where the Ask at one bank is greater than the Bid at another bank. So to implement this method, you would simply need to find two brokers who have different liquidity providers, and place arb-style trades with them.

I create indicator to find good brokers for arbitrage. I attached screenshot. Dots above central line (0.00) represent situation when price on fast feed higher then price on your MT4 broker - BUY signal . Dots bellow central line (0.00) represent situation when price on fast feed lower then price on your MT4 broker - Sell signal . Number of dots shows how big this price difference. Indicator draws green and red dots. Green dots shows current difference on current M1 bar. Red dots shows how smaller current difference on the same M1 bar comparing with previous. Numbers 0/16 show max and minimal price difference on current M1 bar. For example: Difference 5 points appear between fast feed and MT4 broker and price on fast feed higher then on MT4 broker - Indicator draws 5 green dots. On next tick from fast feed difference 7 points - indicator will add 2 green dots = tota; 7 green dots. On next tick from fast feed difference 4 points - indicator will change color for 3 upper dots from green to red. It means that arbitrage situation fades away.

Here is the most up to date version of this method. It has been changed several times since the thread started so I have been asked to save you some time by continually posting the up to date method on this number one post. Fxstu’s Swef System 1. All trades are based on the high or the low of the candle that has just closed. I use GBPUSD but any pair seems to work. 2. If the range of the trigger candle is less than 90 pips then I do not trade it. 3. I use the Daily chart. Before I start a sequence of trades I write down on a piece of paper the equity level in my account. Ideally orders are placed the minute a new candle starts. However as I am not always up at Midnight then the following morning is ok. Longs MACD on yesterdays candle must be above zero. Yesterdays candle must have closed above 10 EMA Yesterday’s close - the LSMA must be green. We place a long at the lowest price of the candle that has just closed. Take profit 100. I only buy if the 10 ema is above LSMA No stop loss, but if the equity on entire account falls 500 below the equity on my piece of paper then I close all trades. Shorts MACD on yesterdays candle must be below zero. Yesterday’s candle must have closed below 10 EMA. Yesterday’s close - the LSMA must be red. We place a short at the highest price of the candle that has just closed. I only sell if the 10ema is below the LSMA. If I have 5 pending trades then I cancel the oldest pending trade. So the maximum number of pending trades is 4. If MACD is above zero then all pending sell trades are cancelled. If MACD is below zero then all pending buys are cancelled. IF LSMA is red then I cancel all pending buys signals – if green I cancel all sells. Each day at 4pm London time I turn on my computer, if my equity is higher than the amount written down on my piece of paper then I close all opened positions and cancel all pending orders. Optional Extra Rule. It is possible to trade Swef by opening two lots every time we get a signal. We close lot number one according to the normal rules. We could set lot number 2 to break even ( optional ) We close lot number 2 when the LSMA turns against our lot number 2 trade.

I have been asked to modify the Swef plan so that drawdowns may be reduced. The following rules should hopefully help : I trade any major currency pair especially GBPUSD and EURUSD. I will only mention buy trades but sell trades are the exact mirror image. I place my orders around breakfast time in the UK. I use the Daily charts ( and the MACD with the normal IBFX settings). I do not use a stop loss, however I see no reason why you should not. Before I start a sequence of trades I write down my account equity. Between 4 and 5 London time I turn on my computer if my equity has gone up I close all open trades. When I am flat or the MACD falls below zero I close all pending trades. A buy sequence of trades begins when the histogram prints the first bar above the zero line. One buy trade is placed as soon as the MACD closes above zero on the Daily chart. Another buy trade is placed every time that the MACD prints a new high on the histogram, when looking for highs I only check back as far as the most recent first bar above bar above zero. I calculate my buy order by looking at the low of the candle that relates to the MACD high. I then look at the previous candles low. So I am now comparing the low of my trigger candle and the low of the candle before it. I make a note of the price of the candle of the two that has the lowest low and deduct 70 pips. For example if the MACD made a new high and the candle above it had a low of 1.9760 then I would look at the previous candle low – which could be 1.9750. In this case I use 1.9750 and deduct 70 pips So I place a buy order at 1.9680 For example if the MACD made a new high and the candle above it had a low of 2.0000 then I would look at the previous candle low – which could be 1.9995. In this case I use 1.9995 and deduct 70 pips So I place a buy order at 1.9925 Each order that I place has a take profit of 100. I am trying to attach a very difficult to trade EURUSD chart to show how the system would operate. The graph that I have attached aims to show the trigger candles. Remember you also have to look at the low of the candle before the trigger before you make a trade. The graph shows buy and sell triggers. From what I can see the new system will never be buying very close to a high nor will it be selling very close to a low. At first glance it is more complicated than Swef 1 but I promise you it is still dead simple.

Very interesting method, Fxstu, many thanks for sharing. First I want to make sure that I understand correctly, then I’ll try to offer some meaningful comment, for whatever it’s worth. Please correct me if/where my understanding is wrong. -- We’re looking at GBPUSD,D1 as a starting point. -- MACD is being used to determine the major trend. MACD &gt; 0 means an uptrend; MACD &lt; 0 means a downtrend. Basically, we’re looking to buy pullbacks in an uptrend, and sell rallies in a downtrend, on the assumption that the tide of the trend will make good our positions. Let’s assume an uptrend. The reverse applies for a downtrend. -- We’re placing a buy limit order at each previous day’s low. So as long as price keeps making higher daily lows, the orders won’t trigger. However, if/when price pulls back below yesterday’s low, we buy. If prices keep pulling back (i.e. our account balance is diminshing), we keep adding a buy order at each previous day’s low. Hence we are effectively averaging down into the uptrend. -- If/when the uptrend resumes, profit will ensue, and the orders will be closed out at pre-determined targets (100 pips for an individual order, or 500 pips across the board, or at the end of a day when the account equity exceeds whatever it was when the first of these orders was triggered). At this point all open and pending orders are closed, and then we start all over again. I’ve had a quick look at the EA results from Zen’s back-test, i.e. the PDF attached to post # 102. First, an aside. I’ve always said that one’s position must include currently unrealized P/L in open trades, and IMHO the equity curve in the PDF illustrates exactly why. The blue line shows the realized P/L only, the green line the total of both realized and unrealized P/L. The unrealized P/L suddenly becomes very real when the account balance hits zero; the blue line rapidly catches up with the green! So how can we improve the method, in order to avert this? Well, there are 9 buy orders (#91,97,98,121,124,126,127,128,132 – the white lines showing their entry dates/levels on the chart below) still open at a time when the sudden downtrend (see blue box at RHS of chart) kills them off. These orders still remained open despite the fact that MACD has been &lt; 0 for around 35 days before the meltdown. If I understand correctly, the method survived until then largely because there were sell orders triggered during rallies in the downtrends that followed, offsetting some of the other buy orders. But the downtrend in the blue box was a sharp move; it contained no rally (which would have caused additional sells to offset the remaining buy orders). Hence the collapse in the equity. IMHO this method breaks many of the "textbook" rules (i.e. averaging down, taking profit as soon as it is realized, while failing to limit unrealized losses). Here are some suggestions, for whatever they’re worth (assumes buys; vice versa for sells): 1. Close all open buy orders when MACD falls below a certain value: when MACD &lt; 0, the uptrend is no longer valid. 2. Don’t continue to buy while price makes lower daily lows. For example, add another buy order only if its buy price &gt; the price of the last buy order (note: it’s still possible that yesterday’s low &gt; the last buy price). In other words, only one buy order per pullback; the trend has to prove itself further before another buy order is added. 3. As an addition, or alternative, to point 2, limit the number of unbalanced buy orders, e.g. number of open buy orders must not exceed number of open sell orders by a certain number (e.g. 5). Excess orders would need to be closed out, at a loss if necessary. 4. Profit could be improved by letting positions run during strong trends, instead of taking profit soon after account equity recovers to its previous level. The PDF result is typical of systems that average down and/or don’t use stoplosses. Eventually a "death sequence" occurs (usually a strong adverse countertrend), so that a few heavy losses undo the result of many small profits. Having said that, Swef trades in the direction of the prevailing trend, which should significantly improve the probability of delaying the "death sequence" – hence the profitable equity graphs in previous posts. IMHO what you have is a highly original way of timing pullback entries (most traders seem to use trendlines, MAs or price action), but I think the risk management angle of Swef needs some serious attention. If I’ve misunderstood, I apologize: please either disregard my comments, and/or make any necessary mental corrections. David

...tsss. Ok, let's go for explanation on the chart of Morrishe. The range: when we are in range, on ichimoku, we can see the price on both sides of Kumo. You must draw support and resistance on higher time frames (m5 and m15, for m1). Then, the best opportunities are the closest to the support or resistance, with respect of the main trend m1. On the chart, two possible entries. The best entry is when chinkou is far from price, and current price far from Kumo. Goishi Osada, the inventor of ichimoku, said that the best timing is near a "number of ichimoku": 9-17-26-33-42-65....etc. These numbers are close to the cyclic of ichimoku. So, after the last high, we count the candles. It's simple. The closer you get to a cyclic number, the better the timing. Finally: a reverse candle pattern, and closure above tenkan. (on stochastique, a divergence, or a crossing Under 20 is nice). With these filters, probabilities are better. It is the same methodology on all time frames. --- 帖子 #13 ---
The breakout: when, how ? Two situations on ichimoku: 1- Breakout with a big candle without wick (or very little), and tenkan is not flat : entry. 2-Breakout with wick, or little candle: we wait the breakout of resistance by tenkan, and pullback. It is the case on the chart. The price should never be too long away from tenkan, otherwise there will be a pullback. Important: for m1, we always look on m5 and m15 the main trend. Don't take breakout against the trend on m5 and m15 because it's often a corrective movement only. eur aud , m1 Attached Image (click to enlarge)

Hi Yousername Very good question. One of the main mistakes of the beginners is to use the same strategy on all phases of the market, all the time, everywhere.The majority of threads posted are illusory: they works only on trend. The market being very often in range, the losses occur quickly. The great strength of ichimoku is to be able to clearly and quickly show the mutations of the price throughout the day. It is one of the only systems to be as responsive to change. This is part of the basis of the ichimoku, it would take too long to explain the recognitions of market phases in ichimoku. But it is clear that there are two types of trading possible: on impulse, and on correction. It is very dangerous to apply the same strategy to both. Moreover, their criteria of recognition are opposed on ichimoku. In impulse trading, indicated by the rules of ichimoku which define a trend, we prefer acceleration, breakout. On the contrary, in correction trading, we will look for pullback, return to equilibrium, and fewer points, but often. Both types of trading require different techniques. We must not forget the type of financial asset, its personal rhythm. For example, the dax does not behave like eur-usd or usd-jpy. The time frame has an influence on technique, timing. There is much to be said. It will require examples, it will be clearer. I will take the time to detail and specify the recognized phases, and the adapted techniques. Hi Yousername The current cloud is a support/resistant: the current cloud....it's the past in fact. The future cloud show us the "momentum on ichimoku". When SSA (senkou Span A) is under SSB (Senkou Span B), the middle point of tenkan/kijun momentum is under the middle point of the last 52 periods. So, it's red. It's a confirmation of the trend on ichimoku. But there's another very important thing with future Kumo: The broader it is, the more the market is pushed to its extremes: the momentum accelerates very strongly, and very often we are witnessing a pullback, or a phase of range. It's an alert.

"Scalping with ichimoku is impossible", "Ichimoku is for Daily or weekly"... The usual speech... Ichimoku is one of the most powerful systems in scalping. There are two principles in scalping: the excesses of the price and the momentum. We do not trade against the momentum, we trade the return towards the momentum. Which means that the price must have made an excess against the momentum. In ichimoku, the momentum is on the side of Kijun and SSB: Kijun above SSB, it is the bearish excesses of the price that are going to be interesting. Kijun is an average. SSB also. It is the return to equilibrium that is traded. A simple example: on the chart, whenever the price moves away from kijun against the momentum, there is correction. A price that makes an excess against the KIJUN-SSB momentum of the upper time frame, there is correction (these are the trades posted yesterday). It is possible for everyone to find a personal way with this phenomenon (pattern, oscillators, break out of trend ....), notably thanks to the price action: an excess of price that test a previous level against the momentum kijun -SSB, is unlikely to succeed in breaking this level ... This phenomenon is valid on all time frames. Dax-m1 (with Ctrader Platform, TS2 from FXCM is closed today): Attached Image (click to enlarge)

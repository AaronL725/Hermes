This is a thread dedicated to the discussion of the Monster Muncher EA, which the illustrious Steve Hopwood has coded and I have designed. All kudos to Steve for a great job on this EA. I personally don't like putting robots on my account, so I only trade this manually. My own interests have been in the manual trading aspects alone, and so that is what I hope to cover here: how to trade MM for the best manually. I have forwarded tested it manually and have done very well on my live account after a little practice on demo. ===================================================== Important : This EA counts according to the GMT offset time of your broker. A broker with a GMT offset time of +2 will give different projections than a broker running on GMT. I use IBFX which counts according to GMT. Any other GMT broker will give close projections to that. I can't recommend using a non-GMT time broker for this system. I only have experience with IBFX. Brokers with a GMT offset time can seriously alter the results. Best suggestion if you have a non-GMT time broker: Get a demo from a GMT time broker and take your signals off that. Important #2 : There is a bug in the EA which causes the charts to offshift every hour. You must refresh the template every hour-this will restore it to its original setting. Now that this info is up top and highlighted, I will be ignoring any further questions about it on the thread. I have written this 5 or 6 times in the thread itself, even though it was already stated below in this first post. I can do no more. One last time: You must refresh the template every hour. Important #3: This is a strategy in development-I am urging people as they learn this strategy to either trade demo, or trade for a penny a point. This is common sense-use caution with your funds. Nothing in FX is certain and all trades involve some element of risk. This is about creating a system which establishes favorable probabilities over time-not a holy grail. It takes time, study, and practice to become successful with any method. ========================================================================================================================= So here is the basic breakdown of the system: First I'll explain the lines. The center Red line, or Average line is the heart of the system. Here is how it is calculated: It calculates the average range of the first 6 bars of the week( point #1), then it calculates the average range of the second six bars of the week(point #2), then the average range of the 3rd six bars of the week(point #3). Then it takes the average of #1 and #2 and produces a fourth point, #4. The it takes the average of #2 and #3 produces a fifth point #5. The red line is a straight line starting from point #4 going through the center of point #5. This gives you the average trajectory of the first 18 bars of the week. It updates automatically every week. Therefore it take 4.5 hours after the start of the week to calculate the red line on the 15 min chart, 9 hours for the 30 min chart to calculate, and 18 hours for the 1hr chart to calculate. The top of the weekly ATR (Blue line) is placed above the Red line. The bottom of the weekly ATR (Green line) is placed below the Red line. The Yellow lines which are very close to the Red Line are not used for signals, just for helping with observation of price action around the red line. They are not essential, but I find them helpful. So, this system can give you four kinds of trades. Buy #1: Price closes above Red line RSI is oversold. Buy #2: Price closes at or below Green line RSI oversold. Sell #1: Price closes below Red line RSI is overbought. Sell #2: Price closes at or above Blue line RSI is overbought. The 1Hr chart is the most reliable chart to trade in my exp. 15 min gives you a few more trades but can cause drawdown. I like to watch 15 min, 30 min, and H1 to see if they are giving me aligned signals, but only take my acutal signal off the H1. You don't have to watch all three TF's, up to you. You can trade just off the H1. ======================================================= Charts, EA, and template posted below. EA goes in your broker&gt;experts. template goes in your broker&gt;templates. Template #1 is already set to disable live trading and alerts. Template #2: also has lives trading and alerts disabled, but has user feedback turned off, and the ADR indicator removed. Its just the lines with the RSI, without any other kind of text. It looks cleaner. Final Note: You have to refresh the template every hour if trading on the 1hr chart, every 1/2 hour if trading on the 30 min chart, every 15 min if trading on the 15 min chart. It will recaculate on each new bar it makes- just a bug-so to restore it to its original setting just refresh the template. Types of set-ups to avoid: I've had a very high percentage of winners trading this system. There is one kind of set-up though which I have been able to isolate which can fool the system. So you will want to avoid this type of scenario. When you have a pair trading in a steep trend, either bullish or bearish, you can get this very steep angle created from the first 18 bars. What can happen then is that a steep trending pair can move accross the chart and the Red line horizontally, so its basically already oversold or overbought at the moment it crosses the Red line. The GU for example this week moved from left to right horizontally across the red line in an overbought state. Then it went bullish "under the red line" triggering a sell and just kept going bullish. Anyway, a picture is worth a thousand words. I have two examples of this. You can spot it easily by the horizontal movement across the Red line and the steepness of the lines. This is the exception rather than the rule, but you should know how to spot these kinds of false set-ups.

I'm just going to cover the basic ranges of SL and TP that I have observed trading this system. Nothing is set in stone here. If you are trading on a type 1 buy or sell, which is close to the red line, for the most part you can place tighter stops if the price crosses the red line in the wrong direction after your trade entry. You might decide to use a 50% level between the red line and yellow line, for example. Most of the time that would work out to be about 30 points. Your min TP on these types of trades is around 50 points. Average realistic TP 75-110 or 120 points, in that ballpark. But then you know I have used stops as big as 50 points to my advantage even on a type 1 trade. So this is to a large extent a matter of personal preference and risk tolerance. Retrace trades tend to pay off a little more-more like 90 to 150 points. So a 45 point stop in this kind of type 2 trade gives very favorable RR. I don't mean to suggest that this type of drawdown is the typical story on most trades. In my experience it has not been-its been a lot smaller. Again, the pair's volatility is a factor. GJ always backtested better with wider stops and tp's than most other pairs because its volatile. Basically, getting all this right is just a matter of practice and observation. I also have to work on holding out for bigger TP's than I've been taking. But thats the general idea.

I just want to clarify a point about waiting for the closing of the bar before you take your trade. It is more essential to wait for a close when it comes to type 1 trades-trades taken near the Red line. The point of this is to keep you out of trouble when price is testing the red line. If price is trading under the Red line for example, this shows an indication that its going to go bearish. At some point its going to test that indication. If it passes that test it will be OB and close under. If it fails that test it will break up through it and close over. Type 2 trades (blue line, green line retrace trades) do not involve these same exact dynamics. In type two trades its just straining its average weekly range plus the OB OS indication. Its not really testing anything. Since this is true, its not as important to wait for a close on type 2 trades. It is still of course very important to wait to see that price has hit or gone below/above green/blue line and RSI is reading 30- or 70+. To summarize: Type 1 Red Line Trades: Very important to wait for close of bar. Type 2 Blue/Green Line Trades: Not important to wait for close of bar, but still important to wait for contact and RSI indication.

--- 帖子 #195 ---
Blue numbers are ultimate fibs. That is a fib from the historical low = 0% to the historical high = 100%. When you have an ultimate fib level correspond with another price level it makes it stronger, especially 38.2, 50, and 62.8 fibs. 50% levels are always important. But the more you slice up the chart by adding more 50% levels, they become less and less important. This is why I color coded them. So you can see that some levels are more important than others. First 50% levels have more pull than say 4rth rank 50% levels. But still 4rth rank might become a support/resistance level and come into play. Any level has the power to stop price and reverse it, just some are more likely to. This why they are ranked by color.

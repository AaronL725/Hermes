This system is very simple but requires the ability to open 20 equal positions. I've been using it on the Euro getting over 5% a week. 1) Choose a direction using whatever method you want or guess it really don't matter. 2) Sometime before or after all the news for the day is released, open your trade. 1 position, no sl, and 50 pip tp, I use 50:1 leverage which gets me about 1% per position opened you will do better with higher leverage just make sure you have enough margin for 20 positions. 3) Come back the next day before or after news releases(same as step 2) and if the trade is still there open another position and move the TP for all open positions to 50 pips from your average position, if it's gone it's gone go back to step 1. 4) Repeat step 3 Even if you choose the wrong direction a small correction will get you your pips when averaged out, I haven't waited more than a week and at one time it was 150 pips away from mt TP. I place my trades around 11:00pm NY time, I use OandA so splitting an account into 20 positions is easy since their minimum contract is $1. Update 8/17/12: I have been opening new trades immediately after my TP is hit, then I add additional positions the same time every day until the TP is hit. It increases profitability. Update 8/19/12 Updated rules in post #91 in this thread. Also all new trades after 8-20 will be split into 2 accounts. Long in one account and shorts in the other.

I have experimented with something similar in the past without ever getting around to making a trade-able system out of it. What I did was to start selling in the fashion you suggest when a stochastic was over bought and start buying when it was oversold. This is to increase the chance that it will revert in the desired direction so that you can collect your profits. Other ideas I have had are: * wait until a pair is trending strongly. If it is in a downtrend then wait until it is overbought (stochastic) then start selling, if it goes against you keep averaging in your price at a set number of pips until you have the required profit. For an uptrend wait until it is oversold. The idea here is that trends persist and that "v-shaped" bottoms are pretty rare and by entering into an obviously trending market and waiting for a pull back you are putting the odds in your favour. * wait until a pair has filled it's ATR. If it's an up-day start shorting, a down-day start buying. The idea here again is that price is overextended and a reversion against the prevailing direction is likely at some stage as traders book profits etc. * Price is constantly testing breakouts. As price makes a new high or low then sell or buy. If the breakout doesn't fail then it will come back to test the breakout. i.e it will revert in your desired direction......at some stage. Not over-leveraging is very important obviously. Just some ideas, hope they made sense to someone.

The problem is that data below 99% is highly inaccurate, so the backtest may not be reliable (which basically never is, but even less so) The only way you can be sure of this is to download better data, I personally use dukascopy data and manage it with a software called StrategyQuant. If I'm not mistaken they have a free version where you can download the data but more slowly. After downloading it you can import it into metatrader and use it instead of the default ones. You can understand the quality of the data from the green bar on the report or from the writing modelling quality. Often there are differences even between 90 and 99%, imagine if it is lower.

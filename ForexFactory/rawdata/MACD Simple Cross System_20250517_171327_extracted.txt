I gave some more thought to this MACD system. I think that it is worthwhile to consider replacing MACD with stochastics. With stochs, we get a better indication of momentum and especially when momentum is decreasing significantly. When the stoch enters the "overbought" zone, it often travels there for a considerable time before coming down. Why is this? Because the price continues making higher highs and higher lows, and the stoch has an upper boundary of 100, so it is IMPOSSIBLE for it to go up more, so it just go sideways in that zone. However, when momentum begins to reduce, the stoch will start down and eventually come out of the zone. This does not necessarily mean that the momentum has REVERSED from up to down, but merely indicates that momentum is DECREASING, so it is not likely that higher highs will continue and should be a reasonable exit point. Unfortunately, there is no specific indicator to tell when momentum changes direction. However, when the stoch crosses 50, that can be used as a signal that MOST LIKELY the momentum is now in the direction of the crossing. However, for signaling when to exit trades, waiting for stoch to cross 50 often has price making a significant move down, resulting in loss of pips. By ending the buy period when the stoch crosses the "over bought" marker, more pips are retained. With this in mind, we can use a stoch with a larger period and look for the crossing of 50 for momentum based direction, then use a smaller period stoch for making entries in this direction, only using the crossings of the %K on the %D. On the attached screen captures, I placed a stoch(5,3,3) on the chart, then added to the same window a stoch(64,9,9). The stoch 64 has only the signal line displayed in yellow. Both have the limits set to 0, 100. This system shows a sell period followed by a NO TRADE period followed by a buy period. So this system does not keep you in the market all the time, but after a sell period ends, it waits on the sidelines until a buy period is signaled. It is a mistake to anchor a specific chart as the absolute chart to determine buy/sell zones for all other charts. This is a mistake that even most experienced traders make; they always use weekly or daily chart to decide direction to trade on all lower charts. There is some specific way, beginning with the biggest chart and working down, of DECIDING which chart is the "control" chart of the trade direction. After the control chart is established, trend trades are made on all lower charts in the direction of the control chart. So, because the control chart is not fixed, it cannot be anchored. However, because the daily chart tends to stay in one direction for about 5 days, it often ends up working out as the control chart. We need to do backtesting to check this. Technically, no, H4 should not be the fixed chart for determining buy/sell zones no matter the timeframe being traded. The "correct" chart is the "control" chart. However, because it would take some educating to understand this idea, we may need to simply use the chart that is 4x the chart being traded to set the buy/sell zones. We have observed, at least on limited data, that the orders opened earlier in window tend to be winners, whereas those opened later, especially the ones near the window close, tend to be losers. In addition, there are an unknown number of add-on trades, and they are only seen when there is a significant retracement. The way it stands now, we wait until their is a momentum drop and only after momentum resumes do we make an add-on. Buy making all of those add-ons by pending orders, we get to reap pips from the strong momentum. And by trailing ALL of the open orders to the same SL value, several of the earliest trades will be in profit, and should be significantly more in profit than the sum of the losses of any of the later trades, resulting in positive pips when the basket is closed.

A trading plan is essentially a crude model of the trading "entity" in which we attempt to identify the more prominent features that appear with some identifiable regularity. And being based on activities of humans, the market WILL have anomalous events (catastrophes, economic news, etc.) that result in a COLLECTIVE reaction of the HUMANS that underlie the market activity that produces market movement that falls outside of the "norms" that were generalized in the model. The model can be refined to attempt to include detection of some of the market action that results from anomalous events, but is it worthwhile to even try? This is why when one develops a trading plan, it is crucial to make a critical analysis of the failed trades, looking for conditions both before and after that occur more often with the bad trades than for the good trades, and modifying the plan armed with this information. In my experience in dealing with traders, the major reason I see for failed trades is not the trading plan, but the trader. Human emotion is a very powerful motivator, and emotions being in charge of the trade instead of the plan being in charge of the trade is the biggest contributor to failed trades. As for trading plans, the biggest obstacle to finding a "good" one is lack of patience coupled with lack of any kind of understanding of probability and statistics. If you don't the have patience to make an in depth analysis of AT LEAST 30 consecutive trades (100 is better) and understand the limits and ranges found by STATISTICAL metrics, you will jump from one "holy grail" system to another, abandoning "yet another crap system" as soon as you get a handful of results that have NO STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE. I am confident that not more that 2 people that are subscribed to this thread even know the difference between a stationary and a nonstationary series and why we would even care about it in trading. Everyone must decide on a stop loss for himself, because it is closely linked to how much money you are prepared psychologically/emotionally to lose without "freaking out". Maybe for you it is $2, or maybe it is $25--only you can know. And you might not know right now what the value is for you. You might THINK you know, but the reality is, when price starts going against you, it will reach a point that you start to get anxious and become afraid that you made a mistake and will end up with a loss. If this point where price is against you is rather far from where you set your SL, then cearly, you are not OK with losing the amount that you set your SL to, and you need to use a smaller SL amount. This demo account that I put in the Trade Explorer was supposed to be for $1000 but it is $10,000. So I trade it as if it has only $1000 in it. I am prepared to lose 1% of that $1000, which is $10. If I trade a single micro lot 0f 0.01 lot, that is 10 cents per pip. That means I can set an emergency SL at 100 pips. Even if I open 2 contracts of 0.01 lots (or a single contract of 0.02 lots), I can set the SL at 50 pips to reach my loss limit of $10. And you can further adjust your trades and use 0.02 lots and set TP at a mere 5 pips, still having SL way out at 50 pips, and harvest the same amount of money as if your trade were 0.01 lots with 10 pip TP. What is the probability of the trade going only 5 pips and hitting your TP versus going 50 pips against you and hitting your SL? Anyway, look at the trades in the Trade Explorer. How much does the price go against me in those trades. How many even went 10 pips against me? If your system has high accuracy of getting you into trades with little drawdown, and the system is only going for a small amount of pips, why worry about placing a SL close by? This system is the inverse of what most systems do. Most systems have a low win rate, use small SL, and rely on winning a few trades with a very large number of pips, and typically, trade on H4 or Daily charts. The 10 pips per day system has a very high win rate, useses a very large SL, and relies on rarely hitting the SL, and can be traded on any time frame. Notice I never once mentioned Reward:Risk in the above discussion.

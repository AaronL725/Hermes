PINBARS ROCK Pinbars are dead easy to spot, and are a most reliable way to spot a good trade entry opportunity in my experience. The pinbar is also known in candlestick parlance as a Hammer, for bullish entries, or Shooting Star / Inverted Hammer, for bearish entries. I use 1-Hour or higher charts to spot potential entries, never lower timeframes. A good long tail, with a small real body, is a prerequisite. Entry To enter, there are two basic approaches: 1. For long pinbars, seek to enter perhaps halfway into the Pinbar, on a re-trace. This is cost-effective, but can mean losing a great trade if the retrace doesn't actually happen in practice. 2. Just go in directly on close of the pinbar. This may cost you more in terms of Stop Loss, but means you don't miss potentially great trades waiting for a retrace that doesn't occur. Stop Loss is always placed several PIPs beyond the tail of the pinbar. Confidence in the entry is increased when you can see confluence with important S+R level(s), for example: 1. Key Fibo level, typically 50% or 76.4% retracements. 2. Significant Fibo extension level, eg 127.2% or 161.8%. 3. Notable S+R level created within the previous 24-48 hours trading. 4. BRN (Big Round Number) levels sometimes, eg x50, x00 levels. Pinbar trades usually offer a very good Reward:Risk Ratio - I would seek a minimum 2:1, often 3:1 or better. After the initial push away from the pinbar entry, you can often lock away your risk to break even quite early in the trade. Managing your risk Of course, by definition, pinbar trades are often counter-trend trades, going against the prevailing trend of the moment, so be fully aware of this before you enter. If you want to minimise risk, choose only those pinbar entries that lead to a resumption of the trend after a retrace. Exit Just choose your own favourite approach, which could be one of the following: 1. A given Reward:Risk achievement, eg 3:1 or greater. 2. Key S+R level, eg 50% or 76.4% Fibo level. 3. Trailing Stop. Here I'll post some examples of pinbar trades on 1-Hour or longer timeframe charts. Please note that I trade primarily on Price Action alone. (I can't deny also keeping half an eye on the 20 (Red) and 50 (Blue) EMAs, to see if they are telling me anything useful.)

It CAN work long term IF one learns to utilize this marker as the tool for what it is....this kind of formation forms when: There is a level of absorption (that we cannot see or detect) usually born of dark pools of interest that "absorb" all the market orders (ie agression). When one comes to recognize these conditions and understand how order flow works in the market these formations indicate larger interest in the market that will likely result in a reversal. THIS is the one we want to find. These formations also form from simple lack of interest in the current direction, ie: NO aggression. No one is willing to hit that market order button...thus price pulls back and the candle closes and "viola"...a pin bar. These are usually losers and often fail after a short and meaningless retrace. There is a certain aspect of discretion involved in almost all trading...even the algos people develop require some discretion in their creation. One very important clue that many leave out here (on FF) is that volume is a good deciding factor in helping your discretion. On the first condition listed above, the deciding factor (absorption vs lack of interest) is volume. Yes, even though its tick volume it is the difference in a likely winner or a likely loser. As well when one learns to recognize the other order flow/volume phases as they progress...the absorption phase is often the last step prior to reversal. This "discretionary" evaluation can result in reasonably achievable goals...60%-70% success rates of at least 2xR targets. Patently untrue. This formation was noted centuries ago by the Japanese who had different names for this formation. The word "Pin bar" was coined here by a FF Famous participant "Merlin". He called them "Pin bars" to designate "Pinocchio" (with the big nose when he lies). It was later shortened to "Pin". However I repeat when used in the proper context this formation is VERY powerful.

I apologize for the late response. I have been thinking this through so as to answer your questions and not distract from this thread. This thread seems to want to focus on price formations (affectionately called "pinbars"). But I think that focus is misguided. While the effectiveness of this formation under the right conditions is undeniable...it is more that just the formation itself. Pinbars (hammers &amp; Shooting Stars) are only one of many price formations (known to many as "price action signals") that are actionable. However the focus on price is as I stated before, "misguided". It is the conditions that cause these formations that is more important and yesterday should've shown that beyond any doubt. These conditions are what I refer to as "context". As I mentioned before. Pinbars can form at the end of a session or between sessions (if you trade intraday). All that really indicates is that in the space of 1 hour (or what ever time frame you are watching)...price went one direction and then the other and finished the period roughly in the same area. That in and of itself means nothing really. Thus many more "learned" traders will say these formations are "useless". And indeed if your focus is purely on price...they are correct. Still others will say that if this forms in an area of support/resistance or a supply/demand zone, the "structure" is telling you price will turn and go the other way. Again, this is observationally correct but misses the point of WHY price reacted the way it did. But this is only a correlative view of the markets action and correlation is NOT causation. If you want to focus on trading reversals...(either back into an existing trend, or reversing entirely)...then you must learn to recognize trend exhaustion. A pinbar isn't that...The pinbar CAN indicate the last phase of trend exhaustion, but in and of itself it is not. It is simply and indication that price when down and up (or up and down) in the span of 1 hour, or 15 min, or 1 day. If one learns to understand WHY price went down and up so quickly it would assist you in making better trading decisions. The only way to discover the WHY of price movement is to understand the underlying market structure. Time, Order flow, and Volume. As we say yesterday, TIME was an important factor in the formation and subsequent failure of several hourly pinbars. What we are looking for in a good trading signal is the final phase of exhaustion, volume absorption. This is when aggressive trading at the market is absorbed by existing pending orders that were placed ahead of time in an area of supply and/or demand. So now to answer your questions: I assume that by using the word "color" you are asking if the pin bar finishes bearish or bullish? No. The importance is that I suspect the pinbar is showing me absorption, AFTER first showing me a climax phase and and exhaustion phase. Obiously if I am looking to buy, I would want to see a pinbar with a long lower shadow and, conversely, if I am looking to sell, I am wanting to see a pinbar with a long upper shadow. The pinbar itself does NOT determine my bias to buy or sell. It is simply the last phase of an exhaustion signal that tells me WHERE the absorption area might be. While I'm on the subject...pinbar formations are NOT the only formations that indicate absorption. But they are the one we are focusing on here.

Hi Until now this would be the way I trade pinbars : This example is in Daily timeframe but can be adapted to others. -I put 2 orders after the pinbar is confirmed : 1. buy stop order at about 12pips from the body of the pinbar. Like this I prevent some losers if price might go lower straight through the hole pinbar. 2. buy limit order at the end of the tail, at about 1/3 of the size of the tail. This order does not get filled so often, but if it does, the risk is small because of SL being close. SL is the same for both orders, a bit further away from the tail of the pinbar to give it some space. I have 6 scenarios : 1* price goes down trough the pinbar at once without retracing. =&gt; I lose with the order #2 which got activited. Order #1 did not get executed. 2* price goes up straight after the pinbar with little retracing. =&gt; I win with order #1. Order #2 does is not executed. 3* price goes down and activates order #2 then it goes up again and activates order #1. At that moment I put order #2 in breakeven. Price continues going up. I win with order #1 and order #2. 4* price goes down and activates order #2. It goes back up and activates order #1. At the moment of activating order #1 I put order #2 in breakeven position. Then price goes back down. I lose with order #1. With #2 I was already in Breakeven and make no loss. 5* price goes down and activates order #2 then goes back up to the base of the pinbar. I put this order in break even. Price goes back down without activating order #1. Then price goes back down and my SL at break even position gets hit. No losses no wins. 6* price goes up and activates order #1. At that moment I eliminate order #2. Price goes back down and touches my SL. I lose with order #1. As you see I can use 2 times my original lotsize, because I never have the 2 orders active with risk. Whenever they are active both, one of them is already in break even position. Of course I watch several other things before operating like : volumes, pinbar big enough comparing to ATR20, must be at S or R level, watch divergences, trend, trend lines, last candlestick on higher timeframe, if there enough space to run to enough benefits, ... As for taking profits, it depends on your favourite way of doing it. A disadvantage is that these valid pinbars on daily charts do not occure very often. Any suggestions are always welcome.

Rules: 1. Wait For A Trend (Color) Change on Non-Lag Dot (Setting 24) (Must Be Following A Closed Bar And Closed Color Change) - Lowest Valid Time Frame For This is 15 Min Chart! 2. Pull Fib Retracement From The Low or High Prior To Trend Change (Depending On The Direction) - Bullish pull from the low - Bearish pull from the high 3. Wait For Price To Pullback To At LEAST 50% Following The Trend Change - If working on a higher time frame drop to 5 min or 15 min 4. Buy or Sell Following A Bounce Off The Fib Levels Entry Rules: 1. After There Is A Reaction Off The Fib Levels. Put A Buy Stop Or Sell Stop At A Set Number Of Pips Above The Low Or Below The High Of The Bounce or Reaction (This is going to create an entry similar to a Renko Chart Without Using Renko Charts). So A Fixed Number of Pips After The Bounce. 2 There are many ways to enter a trade. This is just one idea that I believe is good for this type of setup. Exit Rules: 1. First Profit at -24% 2. Runner or -62% Stop: 1. Above Or Below The Most Recent High Or Low 2. OR NLD (Non-Lag Dot) Color Change Fib Levels - 0% - 100% - 50% - 62% - 70% - 79% - (-24%) - (-62%) I anticipate this will be a bit confusing to many. If you have never used Fibonacci tool, it will take some practice. It should sink in with practice over a couple weeks. I will post many charts to explain. I'll probably do some videos as well at some point. Enjoy

No it is not. In bullish setup the Fib Draw is THE LOW BEFORE THE SIGNAL / COLOR CHANGE. To Point 2, as you reference, which is THE HIGHEST HIGH PRIOR TO A PULLBACK. So the market will show you that level, but you won't know where it is until the pullback starts to occur. As long as, the market pulls back to AT LEAST 50% that will be the referenced high. If the market makes new highs without pulling back at least 50%, you will continually keep drawing point 2 to whatever the highest high that it keeps producing. If it never pulls back 50%, then there is no setup. For example: Attached Image (click to enlarge) Same Chart Pulling To A New High Attached Image (click to enlarge)

Yep, Exactly. You could add 1 Hour---&gt; 15 Min or Even 30 Min---&gt;15 is good or 5 It's not so much conducting multiple time frame analysis. You always want to trade off whatever Time Frame the setup initiated from. For example, I see a setup based on the 1 Hour. Once I see that a pullback is occurring and its approaching 50%, I will drop down to a 15 Minute chart to zoom in, on the highs and lows of the range. It's purely perspective so that you can properly measure your buy and sell stop entries. Once you go down to the 15 Min, in this scenario, I like to remove the NLD indicator because I just want to focus on where my entry is and where I set my stops and targets. The indicator is now useless on the 15 Min because the trade is based on the 1 Hour. Technically yes you can accomplish this on the 1 Hour is well but it's just much easier for me personally to analyze. That being said, I am a big believer in multiple time frame analysis. If the NLD is bullish on a daily chart, odds are that you would have more success with only bullish setups. Just sayin'. There is one other reason for dropping a time frame and that is another entry strategy will I will introduce.

--- 帖子 #28 ---
Line Break charts are always based on closing prices. The general rules for calculating a Line Break chart are: If the current closing price exceeds the previous line's closed price, a new white line is drawn. If the current closing price falls below the previous line's closed price, a new black line is drawn. If the current closing price does not rise above nor fall below the previous line, nothing is drawn. In a Three Line Break chart, if rallies are strong enough to display three consecutive lines of the same color, then prices must reverse by the extreme price of the last three lines in order to create a new line: If a rally is powerful enough to form three consecutive white lines, then prices must fall below the open point of the last three white lines before a new black line is drawn. If a sell-off is powerful enough to form three consecutive black lines, then prices must rise above the open point of the last three black lines before a new white line is drawn.

Hi all, I don't know if anyone's still looking at this thread, but i'd like to say a couple of things about the TLB (3 line break) method introduced here. First of all, I've used this method and STILL use it for stock and option trades for more than 14 years. After a while, I discovered that TLB combined with Aroon, delivered astonishing results. Let me stress out again that I'm talking about stock and option trading and NOT forex. There is one minor problem with the use of TLB (in my opinion), which can easily become a major issue depending on the market and the timeframes a trader is engaged into. The problem is that the TLB signals appear when the new trend is well underway. This means that a trader can lose significant amounts of profits before he/she enters the markets. But this is not all. The greater risk lies when big intraday -or even daily- movements appear with long bodies and tails. This situation is driving the TLB chart crazy, with buy and sell signals appearing even within the span of 2 or 3 bars apart. This can blow any account -without risk management- in a matter of hours!! For example, the DOW index would be a disastrous play for a trader using the TLB due to its volatile nature. And this is a stock index with daily volume at a fraction of the forex market!! Imagine the danger on the FX market where high volatility activity occurs many times even in the same day. What I have written above, does not intend to make you reject TLB. It is a very good tool in any trader's toolbox. But as with all tools, it's not for all market conditions. So be careful how to use it!! ------------------ famagusta

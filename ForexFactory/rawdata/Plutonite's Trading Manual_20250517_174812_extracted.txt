Part A: Mathematics Take 50 pebbles in your hand. Stand on a pavement next to a long ruler, and thrown the pebbles gently with one gesture of your hand. What do you get? On the ruler scale, the pebbles are scattered randomly over a range (forget the horizontal scattering, lets concentrate on one dimension). Now these numbers - the distance of each pebble from you - is a random variable..something which cannot be determined beforehand. But take an average of these distances, and compare the distances from the average..what do you see? I'll tell you, because you are oh-so-busy and won't do it for yourself, you little bum. You will realize that the pebbles are generally closer to the mean distance then any other, because that's the definition of the mean. By the well-known Law of Large Numbers, as you produce more and more pebbles from the same distribution(throw of your hand), the "clustering" around the mean becomes more likely. They converge on their mean, in other words. In fact, probability theory allows us to make some very strong, provable statements about random numbers despite the fact that they are random events. One of these things, called the central limit theory (also very well known to statisticians) tells us that the variance of these pebble distances (or yes, very smart of you, closing prices of candlestick bars..etc) is very limited to the mean. "Variance" is calculated with a simple iterative formula, and the well known and beloved Standard Deviation is just the square root of that. In theory, a normal distribution would have more than 95% of the random vairables within 2 standard devs of the mean. So why is this interesting? Well, think of this..if you threw those pebbles all at once and you slowy took a survey of about 47 out of 50 distances..imagine you found 5 pebbles that were already at 2 standard deviations away from the mean so far. Now before you look at the rest, you can practically rest assured that they will be closer to the mean than the 5 outliers, because of the nature of "randomness". Since you've already reached the normal limit of numbers that have strayed from the mean, the rest should (provably) be closer. Do you see the dollar signs now? Good, now get some more coffee. And for more on this great stuff, please read: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_deviation http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_limit_theorem

With the above articles, we dealt with the market as being completely random, and we made certain statements about random numbers taking into account the ideas of market bias(trend) and changing sentiment, which we will now discuss. To use a standard deviation channel (bollinger band..etc) we have to agree on the number of events/pebbles/candlestick bars to use in our calculations. The more the better, of course, but unfortunately the "distribution" here changes arbitrarily with strong fundamental events, so we usually cannot have a representative set of more than 2-3 weeks of data, i.e 50-60 bars on a H4 chart. For this reason we draw the well-known SMA bands (aka bollinger) with a 55 period (50 is good too). The number of deviations should be 2.0 but we want to get an early warning and catch more trades so we use 1.8. The "Bands" Custom Indicator in the Metatrader platform will let you do this. So what, dear fellow of the trading pits, can we say? What can we suggest while keeping a straight face, and while not sounding like a presumptious group of fools? What in the world can we postulate about a market where hundreds of billions are changing hands virtually, with speculators all confident in their methods yet madly contradicting each other? The answer does not lie in the retail sector, in fact it's pretty safe to go against most traders (who happen to lose on average) and make money if you had that data, and indeed the brokers do this. The answer also does not lie in some abstract, fancy shmancy indicator based system that either lags behind the price action or alternatively makes nonsensical predictions out of it's bottom. In fact, whenever there is room for disagreement, somebody (usually somebody big) will disagree. The more complex your cute little indicator tries to be, the further it is from the world. Nobody sees your pretty lines, no big bank or investment house trades the penny-markets based on your awesome looking chart. For every trader that uses a SMA 12, there are 10 others using an SMA45. For every genius plotting his SAR curves, there is another entering opposite trades based on a MACD 5-20, and for every one of those there are hundreds of pale-skinned news traders biting their nails and entering in the opposite direction. For every long-winded analysis that leads to a short, an equally convincing one wants you to long the long. So where is the truth? Whence cometh the light? The answer is: search for the things that the traders in general, especially the big investors, all agree upon. Find something that is very very simple and therefore very hard to disagree with. There are 3 of these things, I have learned, and 3 only: 1) Strong unexpected news: these send all speculators(including the big money) scrambling, at least in the short term. Strong news and consistent economic failure/success is what shapes the market. 2) Clear support resistance levels (tight ranges..etc). On the majors, where speculative power is usually effective, everybody sees a tight range, or the break of a very recent low/high. "Trendlines" are not really included here, because everybody draws their slanting lines differently and so there is room for disagreement. Human enjoy stupidity, and they will exercise it if given the opportunity. It doesn't matter that your trendline is right when the idiot in some canadian bucket shop can't see what you see and goes against you. With very clear S/R however, stupidity has little room to work in, and that is a true blessing. 3) Fibs fibs fibs. Everybody loves the fibs. --- 帖子 #121 ---
Hi again! Thanks for asking. What I was saying there was that when we want to make predictions on the market, we obviously do not have the future data with us, but we are trying to make a statement about the next few bars' closing prices (which we liken to the distance of pebbles from you, as random numbers produced in one variance-limited set). In theory, even if we consider these closing prices to be completely random, they should be variance-limited to their mean, i.e they can only go so far from the mean before heading back. And as this distance/price difference approaches 2 standard deviations or exceeds it, the probability of reversing becomes 95%. The problem is that we have no way of knowing when the market is going to change it's sentiment/the distribution of random prices which we are referring to, so we cannot make the same mathematically-proven statements about closing prices as we can about the pebbles. If you throw more pebbles with another throw of your hand, the 2 set are not necessarily related, in other words. Still, the information is not useless. It gives a reasonable measure of the market position, and in absence of huge news it can be used very easily. Combined with our understanding of the market, our following of day-to-day market sentiments and some good experience with price action, we can make lots of pips. Hope this helps and.. Good luck.

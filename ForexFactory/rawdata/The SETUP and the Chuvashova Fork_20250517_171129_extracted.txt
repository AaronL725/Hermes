Direction. I have seen a lot of discussions on how to establish the trend on a chart. I have two ways of doing that. First is to use the Alligator. This is quick and visual. If the price is above the Alligator and the Alligator moving averages are spreading apart, then it is in up trend. If the price is below the Alligator and the Alligator moving averages are spreading apart it is in a down trend. Just a glance at the status of the Alligator tells you a lot. When the Alligator is closing and the price is inside the Alligator it is not trending. My second method of establishing a trend direction is based on price action. A chart moves in a series of Higher Highs and Higher Lows until it reverses, then it moves in a series of Lower Lows and Lower Highs. An uptrend is HH HL and a down trend is LL LH. I mostly use the Fractals indicator to locate these points. When a Change in direction occurs, the progression of HH HL changes to LL LH. This gives us powerful information to use. On this chart, I have identified a powerful top and a powerful bottom. (Note: last low on the this chart is labeled as as LL and it should be HL.) Attached Image (click to enlarge) The Top: The HH HL sequence is broken at the E line by a LL. The Bottom: The LL LH sequence is broken at the E line by a HH. There is nothing new here. Head and Shoulders patterns or a Kings Crown are other names for this, but you can see how they are derived from the universal march of HH, HL, LL, LH fractals. Note how the Alligator behaves after the Top and Bottom

How the E line got its name: I needed and easy way to describe tops and bottoms to help identify them. I came up with the acronym SETUP . For a Top: On the left we have a Shoulder and Elbow followed by a Tip. When price breaks Under the Elbow we have identified a reversal. It then pulls back, usually above the Elbow but not always, to a Pull Back. (If price continues and goes above the Tip it is not a Top.) For a Bottom: On the left we have a Shoulder and Elbow followed by a Tip. When price breaks Under the Elbow we have identified a reversal. It then pulls back, usually above the Elbow but not always, to a Pull Back. (If price continues and goes below the Tip it is not a Bottom.) Get into the habit of labeling the SETUP on your charts. After a while it is second nature to check for them before you do anything else. (I have not labelled every SETUP on this chart. Someone will be pointing that out. Good if you noticed.) Once price breaks the Elbow fractal the E line is what controls the market. Combine that price break with what the Alligator is telling us and you have the key to the highway. Technically, accelerator is the histogram showing the difference between the AO and its moving average. Bill Williams is the best to ask that question to as he introduces both of them in his books. The analogy that I like, is if you roll a ball up a sloped roof it starts to lose acceleration before it loses momentum. It can actually be considered to be accelerating against its momentum as it slows. So acceleration leads momentum, it changes direction before the (AO) momentum runs out. Another analogy is a rocket taking off, it accelerates while its fuel is burning then begins to slow (Negative acceleration) however it still moves upward with momentum until momentum hits zero, at which point it starts to fall. Simple answer: AC Three bars in a new direction (color change) or two bars and a cross on the acceleration zero line can be seen as a buy or sell signal. Of course indicators lag but I use them as a confirmation of what I am thinking. If they are wildly different, I pause and check to see why there is a difference. AO is a very powerful indicator in that it tells you if the chart is impulsive or corrective and also which side of the market long short you are looking at - not that we really need that information. AO, AC, the Alligator and fractals are all carefully designed to work together. AO can help you read the Elliott Wave structure and that is a useful, but it requires a lot of study.

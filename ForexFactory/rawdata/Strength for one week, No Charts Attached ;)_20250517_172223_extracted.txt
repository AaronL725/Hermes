Hi Guys, I had a very simple idea two weeks ago. What would happen if I just follow strength of the 8 majors for one week? Let me explain the idea a bit further: Monday morning 8.00 am (Central European Time) I look at the currency strength calculator on this site: Currency strength calculator I don't exactly know how they calculate the relative strength of each currency but at the moment I don't care. For the time period used to calculate strength I choose a period of the last 10 weeks. Then I open trades for every possible combination of the 8 currencies in the direction of strength, i.e. If CHF is rated stronger then GBP I open a Sell GBP/CHF trade. During the London and US session the open trades will develop into losers and (hopefully) winners. At 20.00 pm (Central European Time) when the markets have settled a bit I look at my list of trades and close half the size of the losers. (If total amount on the negative side is € 800,- I close the largest trades until the negative side is lower then € 400,-). I repeat the last procedure at 20.00 pm of every following day until friday when I will close all open positions. Last week went very well and ended with a nice profit (Demo offcourse). I thought I would share this idea with you to see what you think of this simple approach. Best Wishes and Happy Trading, FBForex

Hi Tutsola, thank you for your input. You could use a fixed stop loss and take profit but I think this will limit the profitability of the system. If you look at the weekly candles on a chart you will notice that the bodies of the candle are quit large in comparrison tot the wigs. This means that during the week price is likely to follow the same general direction. In fact, profits are likely to come from a few open positions with ongoing trend while the other positions are going to be losses or turn out to go nowhere. A fixed stop could mean that during a volatile week (with huge trending opportunities) your positions could be stopped out before they start to follow their initial trend direction again. I try to follow the old wisdom: "Cut your losses and let your profits run"

Hi Lou, welcome to this thread (and others I may not have welcomed yet in this thread). The reason why I started using this with only a 10 week period strength calculation is because it seems a relevant time period compared with one week of trading. I have seen lots of systems which use multiple periods to confirm the readings but I honestly think that you can get as much confirmations as you would like for past price information but you still won't be able to predict price movement in the week to come. Let me put this another way. For instance, how sure are you that, when for the last 10 weeks EUR was stronger then USD and you know that in the last week of that 10 week period strength was also in favor of EUR this will be the same in the following week? Some might argue that because it was moving in line with strength last week surely it will be a corrective move next week. On the other hand one might argue that because last week wasn't in line with strength of the past 10 weeks, chances are greater that next week will be inline with strength.... - I closed trades each day at 20:00 Central European time, you did not. Pay particular attention to Friday of last week. Almost every trade retraced from the time you closed until 20:00. - As stated, I used a different (although, I would have to assume similar) currency percentage calculation. For last week, all of my trade directions were the same as yours except EURCAD. - I'm backtesting using H1 data, fixed spreads , and no swap. Why H1 data? Because the strategy calls for all trading decisions to be made at the top of the hour (08:00, 20:00). So H1 data is sufficient for these purposes. Why fixed spreads? Because MT4 does not store historic ask prices. So, I chose "conservative" spreads for calculations. I could have used historic tick data from a different source, but that adds complexity to the code, takes longer to process, and is not necessary for these tests. Why no swap? Because it is really not necessary at this stage (trying to get a general idea of past performance). If these backtests start to show more positive results, then further backtests could be "fine tuned" to be more precise. This system is not at that point yet. Backtesting is not perfect and has never been advertised as such. However, it serves a very important purpose - to judge system performance under different circumstances in a very fast manner. Forward testing of the same system would take much, much longer.

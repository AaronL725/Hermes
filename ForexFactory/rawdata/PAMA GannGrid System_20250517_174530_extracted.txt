Gann Grid concept Gann grids are used by traders to: - analyze strength of trends; - predict trend changes. Gann grid is used for squaring up time and price. It is based on the Gann line, the 45 degree or 1:1 Gann line, which shows a strong long-term trend. It is an easy to follow indicator for: - investors, - long-term traders, - swing traders. Gann grid is a set of 45 degree lines plotted over a price chart. Price above one of these ascending lines (better if it touches it in one or more points): strong bullish movement. Price below one of these descending lines: bearish movement . Intersection of one line: the basic trend is broken Price intersects more than one line : beginning of a new trend . Traders can enter long positions when the price break above an ascending line. Traders can exit trades when prices break below a descending line. Traders should also use: -Fibonacci tools -Volume indicators to confirm trend strengths and changes. eTrader,

In Gann Grid there are two types of lines. Finding Zone - Positive Line (low to high) Set a positive line at the last valley and monitor if market price will break this positive line. At this point where the positive line would be broken, the sell zone is starting. - Negative Line (high to low) is setting at the last high peak and the buy zone starts for if the market price will break through the negative line. This zone shows us if the market is either in the buy zone or in the sell zone. It doesn't mean to make a market order or execute a trade at this "line" of entry area. Definition Lower-High (L-H): Curent peak price is lower than last peak price. (Often seen in the Sell Zone) Higher-Low (H-L): Current valley price is higher than last valley price. (By a majority in the Buy Zone) See further more details: http://www.forexfactory.com/showthre...ght=GGG&amp;page=2

Gann Angles are drawn between a significant bottom and top (or vice versa) at various angles. The most important by Gann, the 1 x 1 trendline signifies a bull market if prices are above the trendline or a bear market if below. Prices should then be expected to fall to the next trendline (the 2 x 1 angle). As one angle is penetrated, expect prices to move and consolidate at the next angle.

Gann angles, originally known as Geometric Angles, are some of the major tools used by W.D. Gann to profit from the market. The angles can be used to: - find support and resistance levels; - analyze the strength and direction of trends; - predict price reversals. Gann angles are drawn from high or low points of a trend and are representations of time to price relationships. There are 9 major Gann angles of various degrees, which represent fixed rates of speed of trends. These angles are figured out as units of Time x Price (T x P), pronounced as Time By Price. The 9 angles are: 1) 1 x 8 = 82.5 degrees 2) 1 x 4 = 75 degrees 3) 1 x 3 = 71.25 degrees 4) 1 x 2 = 63.75 degrees 5) 1 x 1 = 45 degrees 6) 2 x 1 = 26.25 degrees 7) 3 x 1 = 18.75 degrees 8) 4 x 1 = 15 degrees 9) 8 x 1 = 7.5 degrees Gann angles offer best results when used in weekly charts. They can be used in any time-scales but for accurate results traders should always keep the correct time by price proportions. Gann angles provide significant market insights. - High degrees of angles represent swift trends and low degrees represent small trends. Usually these trends are less reliable and less durable. - During strong up-trends the price stays above a significant angle (1x2, 1x1 or 2x1). Similarly during a downtrend price stays below a significant angle. - If one angle is penetrated (price reversal) the price tends to fall to the next less steep angle (e.g.: from 1x1 to 2x1). - 1x1 is the single most important angle, which gives a 45 degree angle, showing a perfectly balanced trend (up or down). Whenever the price broke this angle a significant trend change is indicated. - When Gann angles crosses a significant pivot point or other past angle then there is a higher change of trend changes. One thing to keep in mind with mt4 is that the chart scaling on the left side of price is different than on the right. So what can look like a nice geometric pattern projecting into the future will eventually get all messed up as time advances. It's usually not that big of a deal if you either pro grammatically re-adjust or manually re-adjust. But either way, it is an issue if you're trying to nail down a time/date in the future for a projected turn. Another thing that has to be considered is the weekend. If you calculate off of trading bars only, it's not an issue. But once you switch to actual time it is an issue.

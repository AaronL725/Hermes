Anyone who wants to learn more about trading TDST bounces please click on this link DeMark TDF trading is a combination of the TD Setup formation and my own thrust method. I am not claiming I am the only person to think of trading this way but as I am starting the thread I have named it TDF with the “F” standing for my surname Fogarty for illustration purposes, who knows, if it takes off perhaps I will get my name in a book, however that is not my intentions with this thread. I am a big believer in looking at the bigger picture then zooming in for entry and that is how I want to structure this thread. I will do daily analysis and try to pick the best opportunities and post updates as setups appear and trades are entered. I think that should cover everything so before I bore you too much I will move on to the TDF method. Indicators: TD Count TF: Higher timeframe for directional bias, lower timeframe for signal and entry Signal: TD Setup followed by a thrust bar Entry Method: Close of first thrust bar, entry can be staggered if risk is too high. If first entry is quickly stopped, take the next thrust bar, MAX TWO ENTRIES PER SIGNAL Stoploss: Use the thrust bar for stoploss using the “show data” function in the TD Count file Risk: Recommended 1% per entry, max 2% per signal Targets: See post number 87 for detailed description Trade Management: Risk can be spread by entering at better levels after the closure of the thrust bar by either using a lower timeframe or when the risk suits I have added illustrations of both buy and sell TDF setups which should be clear although if there are any questions please feel free to ask. You are not required to use my long-term analysis to trade this method but I do recommend having an overall picture before entering a trade. I myself have not traded this method yet but will be trading it live so wish me luck. Here is my take on it: These are the timeframes we are looking at aily, 4hr, 1hr, 30min, 15min and 5 min. Let us suppose we are looking to trade the TDF on daily and we want to zoom in to lower timeframes to enter. My take is that once the bearsih thrush or bullish thrust is confirmed it would be better to zoom in first to 4hr rather than move to 1hr or lower... The reason is that there are always conflicts as far as signals are concerned on different timeframes, so we must do it in a stepwise fashion and not jump between timeframes. So fisrt we must move from daily to 4hr, then 1hr and so on as we have seen on GBPJPY example Bearish thrust on daily completed but 4 hr indicated a buy instead of a short so moving straight to 15min or 30 in chart woud most likely result in a stop out. So what I am currently doing is watch the 4 hr chart TD count upside or any sign of bull exhaustion to zoom in again to 1hr to enter short. So if I want to trade TDF on 4hr charts, will zoom in on 1hr and enter on30 min , if watching TDF on 1 hr would zoom in to 30 min and enter on 15 or 5 min charts.However one can still enter on 5 or 15 min charts even if trading daily but one needs to expect a few stopouts, its a trade off that one needs to make. Good question FXSnipe, In similar fashion to the TDST method from my last thread the TDF setups provide the trader with two simple exit options: TDF Buy Setup: 1. Closest TDST resistance level 2. Trail the stop along the Low of subsequent TD Sell Setups 9th bar Attached Image TDF Sell Setup: 1. Closest TDST support level 2. Trail the stop along the High of subsequent TD Buy Setups 9th bar Attached Image (click to enlarge) Although the recommended exit options, the two simple methods explained above can be mixed up and are not restricted to one timeframe. For example, if the trader takes a TDF buy setup on a 15 minute chart on the same day as we have a TD buy setup on the daily chart the trader could exit half the position using one of the two above methods on the 15 minute chart and hold the remainder of the position to use one of the above methods on the daily chart for bigger reward. Additionally, the trader can mix things up on the same timeframe as I have illustated in the picture below. The trader could exit half the position at the closest TDST support then exit the remainder of the position by trailing the stop at the high of the 9th bar of subsequent TD Buy setup. Attached Image (click to enlarge) As you can see the actual exit methods are straight forward but it is up to the trader to do the analysis and decide whether there is potential to get more from the trade than the entry timeframe provides. --- 帖子 #113 ---
Hello DJ, The levels you are looking at are daily and weekly thrust supports turned resistance. I use daily, weekly and monthly thrust levels to decide when it is appropriate to trade a specific pair. If you mark the first and last thrust bar in each sequence then you will see how the price reacts to these levels, the higher the timeframe the more important. For example, you may find that we have three bullish thrusts in a row then price tests that last bullish thrusts low a few times before finally removing it to a close. Once it is removed to a close that now provides you with your resistance level and you then zoom in to lower timeframes and look for opportunities on approach to this level. Just bring up a plain candle daily chart and mark these levels and see how price reacts to them and you will quickly train your eye to spot them, its quick simple really but very powerful. Hope this helps, Cheers Foggytrader

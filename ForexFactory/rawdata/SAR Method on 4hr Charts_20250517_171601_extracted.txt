Hello. If you read Expectancy Management I explain, with schemas and equations, why this is not a good idea at all for a system to take partial profit. Especially for systems with a big R:R like trend followers. Below is an example of such a short trade that would have reverted to the initial SL --completely cherry picked --. Its lowest low was at ~450 pips from the entry (blue line). If you close half at 200 pips and trail the SL to 0, you get 100 pips net. I think it's not really acceptable to let a 200 pip trade rewind to the zero line or to get only 100 out of 450. On the other hand we shall let some room for the price to move. We need a trade-off. In the picture below (G/U H4) I attached an EMA of the highs and the same EMA of the lows. Here it is EMA 60. There is no need to optimize the period. The distance between the two lines makes a quite good estimator of the volatility. It is smoother than the ATR. One can see the volatility as the amplitude of some market "noise". If the price moves away a larger distance it is statistically valid to assume that the move is real because it went further than its confidence interval. I propose you to trail the SL this distance away from the 4th line in addition to your SAR rule. The white line reports the distance. In this particular case the trade would have closed for 250 pips. It won't always be better, especially when the price spikes a lot before closing inside the yellow lines. But I think it's good in the long term. Another idea is to use a higher TF, like daily, and use the two previous bars highest/lowest as long as it is outside the yellow lines area. This way we more or less trail from S/R to S/R. That's the grey line in the picture, 285 pips from the entry. I don't know which is better. Perhaps they may be combined. Attached Image

Once I see a large range forming on a larger time frame I will then move down to a smaller time frame and try to exit at the range extreme that gives me the greatest profit. From here I will stay on this time frame and trade inside the range, (as this time frame will be trending between the two extremes. ) Once the range breaks (hopefully I am in on the ride from the other side of the range), I will move out to the larger picture again and ride the next trend.

gspajon, Good to hear your feedback and they are all issues that I agree with. They basically all revolve around the same issue, and that is a ranging market, or the higher highs and lower lows (trumpet pattern) scenario. A few ways around this issue as you don't really know when this is going to happen, and you don't want to miss the big move when it does happen, whether that be a news release or just the pair breaking out of a long tight range for whatever reason. First of all, plenty of manual back testing. Get on the charts with your lines and your own rules and test it out over a decent amount of time. Record losses and wins and see what you end up with. Try different variations of your rules to get different results. I have gone stir crazy doing this over the years. Choose pairs that do have some volatility and that experience the big moves on a regular basis. Something like the GBP/NZD, but if you want to watch paint dry, then trade the EUR/GBP. And taking this a step further, be more aggressive in your SAR/stop placement on certain pairs and give other pairs room to move. This is especially the case if some pairs you are trading are moving in correlation at the time eg big news out of the UK, then all GBP pairs may be on a big move but you are not sure if it will reverse, you may want to tighten up the GBP/JPY but not be so tight on the GBP/USD. Another way would be to use a hard stop instead of relying on the SAR to get you out. For example, you have had a pretty decent move on one pair but your SAR is still a long way away, then just put a hard stop in a place you are happy with and keep the SAR as a reversal point only. If you are then stopped out, then continue to move the lines as you normally would and eventually you will get a reversal trade. Don't worry if the trade then continues in your original direction as you should be happy with your profit anyway. And finally, use some other indicator/s or method to help you determine if it is a ranging market and just stay out of the market with this method if that is the case.

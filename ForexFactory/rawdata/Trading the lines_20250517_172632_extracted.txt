This argument may seem to have some merit, but i dont have any good empirical evidence to back up this claim. There are rational evidence to falsify this idea though, and i will get to that below. It does not much matter exactly WHAT logic they have perhaps, sufficed to say for these reasons, lets suppose IF price is driven by a logic that does NOT operate on closing on profits, then that means the trader must assume the same logic. The trader must assume the same logic and use it in action to trade, to place positions and to close. This logic falsfies the TSMP. This argument means that position is not "smart" but instead "other". 2) If the first idea is not correct, and instead there are smart people moving the money smartly, they might still not do it ALL the time. Meaning sometimes stupid or semi stupid people might be moving the market and sometimes the smart people might be moving the market. Actually there are some empirical evidence that falsifies this view, but none that upholds it: It is well known and easy to prove that most investors in currency loose money. From what i have seen maybe 35-20% of accounts does NOT loose money. 35-20% is a lot less then half. Which means that the people who are making profits is much less. Meaning there are LESS smart people then there are dumb people. The dumb people who loose most of the time are knocked out after a while, its not very strange to assume that, theyre funds run out and they are forced to quit. But here comes the thing that falsifies this partly and the above idea 1) in a greater way: If it was NOT the smart money that moved the market at least some of the time, smart money with winning account histories would not exist and would not persist over time. To me its almost impossible to try to imagine the owners of these account operate on a logic that is NOT systematic. And this leads us to idea number 3) 3) The TSMP is what moves the money at some chosen or most often time. There ARE very smart investors that DOES operate on trying to close on profits. They might not always succeed and so on, but the logic of WHEN they do must be derived from the charts, since they are looking at the same charts. However many of them probably rely on tick data and pretty complex EAs that does complex operations on the numbers. But thats ALSO only a hypothesis. There might STILL be some smart money that DONT rely on fancy hardware and software like that. Its quite possible that the TSMP is a product of added reasons to trade. Such as: General D1 downtrend + selling at or above an important recent low + selling at an important recent high = enter the TSMP, at exactly where it happend. TP = 37. So then, in that case its only a matter of figuring out the defenitions of "important recent low" and "important recent high". I have tocuhed on this technique earlier, but maybe not explained it clearly enough. Ok so here it is: Going up and going down Attached Image (click to enlarge) The most simple form of SELL is in a candle thats either GOING DOWN or one that is VOLATILITY. The most simple form of BUY is in a candle thats either GOING UP or one that is VOLATILITY. You should prefer not to keep a SELL position that indicates a BUY and vicer versa. However, a candle that is VOLATILITY can, like this 6th candle with a green square around it, first break the high of the former candle and AFTERWARDS break the low of the candle before it. A candle that takes such a shape is sort of more a SELL indication then a BUY, since price has already been rejected at the top.

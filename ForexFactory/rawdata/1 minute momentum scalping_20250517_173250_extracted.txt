Some things that I remember about VSA: Beware of volume spikes because they are a good sign of supply/demand hitting the market. When price nears resistance from below and volume dramatically increases, this could mean supply is hitting the market. Increasing volume at resistance accompanied by narrowing spreads (of the bars) also shows selling pressure. The big players can also "test" the market. An example (as I understand testing) would be when they mark price down to see if any bears are around. When you see a bar that dips down into a price level where there has been selling in the past, only to close on or near its high, and on low volume, this could indicated that there is no supply left (no more bears) and that price is ready to rise. No demand/supply bars are bars that are reaching into new high-ground with very narrow spread (price range of the bar) on very low volume can be an indication of no demand. I can't remember where price is supposed to close on this I'll definitely find out soon. I read a post recently that compared VSA to learning a language. If the stuff I said above is correct, then you can consider those like phrases of a language that a non-speaker of that language may know. This is different from being able to speak the language. This kind of analysis requires you to interpret lots of things about a chart before you start to draw conclusions based on the types of bars that I mentioned. Everything needs to be in the correct context, and this is where it gets tricky. If anyone comes across this post and happens to know some VSA please I encourage you to correct me and share ! Also another note about VSA. An analogy Tom Williams made in Master the Markets was think of price as a car, and the volume as the accelerator. If price is trying to drive up a hill but the accelerator is not being pressed down, the car will most likely not make it. So when price is steadily rising while volume is steadily dropping, you can assume that smart money is not behind the move. The way I think of it is to first identify the direction of price: up or down? Next, to find the validity of the direction I look at volume to see if it is going up or down. If volume is decreasing, I take that to mean that the direction of the price (be it up or down) is invalid. If volume is increasing, I take that to mean that the direction of price (be it up or down) is valid.

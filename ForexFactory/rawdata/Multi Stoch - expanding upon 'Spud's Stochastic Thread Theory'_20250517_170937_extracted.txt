A LITTLE BIT ABOUT STOCHASTICS Next, let’s revisit the basic concept of Stochastics and why they are so powerful, and have been a mainstay of the many indicators available to us for over 60 years. George Lane, known as the “Father of Stochastics”, developed the formula for this indicator in the 1950’s, and successfully traded it until his death in 2004 at the age of 83. Lane surmised that a change in momentum preceded a change in price direction, using the analogy that if a rocket was hurtling away from the Earth, at some point it would need to slow down before changing direction and falling back to Earth. Stochastics offer us an insight into changes in momentum, and having a good understanding of Stochastics offers revealing insights into changes in market structure and price behaviour. In this way, Lane referred to Stochastics as a “leading” indicator. Available for download here is a great article on George Lane which I recommend people to read. It is an interview with Lane which explains a bit of the developmental history of Stochastics, its core principles, how Lane used it in identifying divergence and how he exploited this set-up every day to make his living.

MULTIPLE STOCHASTICS – A NEW FRONTIER? Next let’s look at the basic concept of Spud’s Stochastic Thread Strategy . Spud decided on adding 19 Stoch K% lines together, overlaid in the same indicator window, ranging from a fast-moving 6/3/3 to a slow-moving 24/3/3. There have been various versions of this set-up floating around, some colouring the fast-moving group one colour and the slow-moving group another, some colouring each individual line differently. The overall appearance of the indicator is similar to the Multiple MA “Rainbow” that many traders would be familiar with. And whilst it does have some similarities to this, at least in appearance, there are some very fundamental differences in behaviour. The MA Rainbow utilises many Moving Averages to clearly illustrate the potential start of a trend by initially having all MA lines “bunched” together and then “fanning out”, the fast moving MA’s separating away from the slower-moving MA lines. An obvious visual separation between the fast and the slow is understood to mean there is a decent trend. When they start to converge again and intertwine, we understand the trend is over and the market is ranging again. With the Multiple Stochastic indicator, we see a noticeable difference in behaviour. When there is a strong trend developing, there will often be a “bunching” of all Stochastic lines, forming into what Spud called a “ROPE”. All individual Stochastic “threads” become intertwined into a visible rope and they travel together, generally from an overbought to an oversold zone, and vice-versa. So we see there is a far different behaviour with the multiple Stochastic when there is a strong trend, as all threads band together rather than fanning apart, as in the MA Rainbow example. When I finally realised the basic brilliance behind Spud’s idea, I decided to try and code an indicator which just offered all 19 Stochastic lines in one instance, rather than having 19 individual instances of the Stochastic appearing in the indicator settings window. What started out as an innocent project however, became an obsession with needing to “unlock” the workings of this indicator, add in some alert functionality, develop a strategy to trade it successfully…and so on and on it went. Over 6 months later and here we are. Although I need to reiterate the fact that what I have made available here is only BASED ON Spud’s initial findings. I have developed his idea into my own strategy, which encompasses other indicators in order to make the strategy consistently profitable. With the original indicator, there are behaviours which were not understood by Spud, which he freely admits in his .PDF file. It is these behaviours which I have spent time analysing, and in doing so, I have identified the need to add in additional filters and smoothing techniques to ensure trades are not entered into unnecessarily, and existing open trades are not closed unnecessarily early. COMMON TERMINOLOGY and INDICATORS USED We are getting close to introducing the strategy itself, but first lets get an idea about some of the common terminology that I will be using. - Each individual Stoch K% line will be called K1, K2, K3 up to K19, where K1 = 6/3/3 and K19 = 24/3/3; - K AV refers to the averaging of all 19 Stoch K% lines; - FAST K AV refers to an averaging of the FAST K group, K1 to K10; - SLOW K AV refers to an averaging of the SLOW K group, K11 to K19; - D refers to the D% line. The default value is 12 , but can be altered in the settings; - ROPE refers to the banding together of all individual Stochastic threads into a single unit, which then travel together in the same direction in a tight-knit fashion. When they begin to separate the rope can be seen as losing momentum. The basic formula I have arrived at suggests that a ROPE is valid when (( K Range ) &lt; X ), where X is a user defined value. The default setting for X is 1; - K Range refers to the value differential between K1 and K19, which is useful when determining whether a "ROPE" is valid or not; - OBX refers to the Overbought level line, where X is a user-defined value, default is 80 ; - OSX refers to the Oversold level line, where X is a user-defined value, default is 20 ; - C1 and C2 representing Candle 1 (previous closed candle) and Candle 2 (current closed candle). THE INDICATORS USED Next, lets examine the indicators that are included in the strategy, and their function. 1) The multiple Stochastic. This is just the plotting of all 19 K% Stochastic lines, 6/3/3 to 24/3/3, and the D% signal line (default value 12 ). All lines are individually coloured to offer an easy visual roadmap. 2) The 20 Period LWMA Bollinger Band I am using the 20 period Linear Weighted Bollinger Band centre line (BBCL) to assist with trade entry and exit. If price breaks above this level, then one out of several trade entry / exit criteria are met. 3) The 20 period Smoothed MA I am using a 20 period Smoothed MA to assist with trade exit. If the data value of this MA shows a change in direction during a trade, then one out of several exit criteria are met. 4) Pivot Points and the Daily Open line Pivot Points and the Daily Open line are mainstays of any strategy that I use. Pivot Points are a great way of targeting a potential Take Profit level, as well as filtering out a trade when price is already in close proximity to these levels, i.e. I would not BUY when price is immediately below a PP. The Daily Open operates in the same way, however it also offers additional sentiment, i.e. if price is below the Daily Open, I may choose to only consider SELL trades. These are the basic indicators that are used in the strategy, and the interaction between all of these form the basis for BUY and SELL alerts, which we will cover next. THE STRATEGY - BUILDING A CASE FOR ENTRY AND EXIT (N.B. The following strategy setups / formulas are for BUY trades only). There are various trade set-ups that are critical when identifying when an entry is valid or not. Essentially, we must consider ourselves as a Prosecutor in a Court. In order to win, we need to slowly and carefully build a case. And to build a successful case, we need evidence. So essentially, this strategy is all about building a case to enter a trade, and the evidence we are searching for comes to us automatically by way of a series of alerts as certain trade entry criteria are met. The evidence we need to build a case on for trade entry are: 1) When C1 ((SLOW K AV &lt; D) AND C2 (SLOW K AV &gt; D) This initial alert identifies the first piece of evidence we need, where all Stochastic threads are grouped in an oversold zone on one candle, and then by the next candle close the SLOW K group has risen above the D signal line. VISUAL ALERTS: - Vertical WHITE line in the indicator window - thickness = 1 This suggests a change in momentum from bearish to bullish, but we are not going to win a case on this piece of evidence alone. 2) SLOW K AV &gt; OSX This second alert identifies the second in the series of setups, where the SLOW K group has risen above the Oversold level line. This is further confirmation of bullish momentum building, but again it doesnt solve our case. VISUAL ALERTS: - Vertical GOLD line in the indicator window - thickness of 2 3) When D &gt; OSX The third piece of evidence appears when the D signal line rises above the Oversold level line. This is a great piece of evidence, and we are in a winning position, but we still need more KEY evidence in order to win our case for entry. VISUAL ALERTS: - Vertical DARKORANGE line in the indicator window - thickness of 3. 4) The ENTRY criteria. There are a number of pieces of evidence required here, so lets break them down one at a time. - (KAV &gt; 50) This suggests that the K AV is climbing and has breached the mid-level 50 line. Stochastics are closely related to the RSI in the respect that if the K% value crosses the 50 line it signals bullish momentum. - FAST K AV &lt; OBX This suggests that the FAST K AV group has not travelled beyond the Overbought level line into the Overbought zone. Doing this would taint our evidence, and invalidate a BUY ENTRY setup. - PRICE &gt; Bollinger Band centre line (BBCL) The current price level must be above the centre line of the Bollinger Band. The Bollinger Band utilises the 20 period LWMA for its calculation. - C2 BBCL &gt; C1 BBCL The current candle close Bollinger Band Centre Line (BBCL) value must be greater than that of the previous candle. We do not want to be buying into downward momentum, which is suggested by a downward sloping BBCL, regardless of what all other signals are telling us. VISUAL ALERTS: - Vertical line LIME (BUY) or RED (SELL) in the indicator window - thickness of 5. TIME TO PROVE YOUR CASE FOR ENTRY! That is all the evidence you need to be able to win a case for trade entry. It may seem like a lot, and perhaps it is, but no case in history ever deserved to win without first crossing all the T's and dotting all the I's. Just like a Courtroom however, not all cases are going to win. There are factors that go on behind the scenes that we may neglect to consider and all the evidence we collect may not be enough to cover everything required to secure a successful outcome. One of these factors is what is happening on a higher time frame, but we will get to that later on. In short, losing cases is part of the life of a Lawyer, just as losing the occasional trade will happen to every Trader. At the very least our strategy needs to be designed to give ourselves the best possible chance of winning, and at the same time mitigate the potential for loss. This means identifying setups that are more likely to be profitable, and trading these rather than setups which are less likely. Over the longer term, the entry criteria listed here for this strategy should outperform a scenario where one or more pieces of evidence were missing. ROPE ALERTS Hey all, time for a few more ramblings. Before I start on the next section however, just a quick note to explain (if it wasn't already obvious) that all of what you read here has been coded into an indicator that offers full functionality, i.e. most settings are user definable so you can alter them to suit your own trading style, or change the look to suit your own visual preferences. Alerts can be changed and switched between a combination of screen pop-up, sound, email and push. Line colours / styles / thicknesses are able to be changed, arrow colours and sizes can be changed etc. Even some of the logic can be changed slightly to allow some minor modification for testing purposes. And of course everything can be switched off so you just trade the strategy manually without alerts, if that is your preference. Getting back to the strategy... Anyone who has read Spuds Stochastic Thread Theory on the FF forum (everyone needs to!) will understand that the ROPE is one of the key principles he talks about. When all Stochastic threads bunch tightly together and travel in the same direction, it presents a potentially powerful signal that deserves our attention. Think of it as strength in numbers - 19 individual Stochastic lines, all agreeing on a direction and banding together to get there. With this strategy there is an in-built ROPE alert function in the indicator. The definition of a ROPE, as I have established it for this strategy, is: ROPE when the value of K Range is less than X, where X is a user defined input value, the default being a value of 1. (NOTE: this is particularly powerful when in an OB / OS zone, but may still present a trading opportunity outside of these zones. Please note also that I am acutely aware that my definition is purely subjective and is open to criticism, therefore if anyone can offer a better suggestion, please PM me and we can discuss further). The default value is able to be changed for testing purposes, as a ROPE may have a value of "5" and still be as good of an opportunity as a ROPE with a value of "1". This definition recognises the bunching together of all Stochastic threads, and an alert is raised when the individual data value of all K% lines are either equal or at least very close together. Different Rope Alerts There are two ROPE CAUTION alerts available, one for when a rope has formed inside an extreme zone and another for when a ROPE has formed outside of an extreme zone, and there is also a ROPE TRADE alert for when a ROPE has left an extreme zone. 1) - ROPE forms outside extreme zone areas: If a ROPE formed outside of an extreme zone, i.e. ABOVE the 20 line and BELOW the 80 line, then it is a potentially powerful signal. This is more of a "heads up" alert, meaning it is time to analyse the market for a potential entry as something is about to happen. The formula is very simple: (K Range &lt; "X") VISUAL ALERT: - text on the screen: ROPE HAS FORMED - CHECK FOR POSSIBLE ENTRY This is particularly useful when using multiple time frame analysis, i.e. if a rope has formed on the hourly chart and you receive this alert, then it is worth checking out potential entries on smaller time frames. 2) - ROPE forms inside extreme zone areas: It happens regularly enough that when a rope forms in an Overbought zone (using a BUY trade example), price has achieved a seemingly unstoppable level of momentum and continues its journey upward, even though the Stochastic indicates severely overbought conditions and we should be looking at a potential SELL. And of course the opposite is true for a SELL. Even with a normal Stochastic it is common that the K% line will continue along in an extreme zone for many bars as price continues its trajectory, perhaps criss-crossing with the D signal line many times. Because of this we need to use caution and only trade when we are certain of the direction. One way of collecting the evidence we need to overcome any uncertainty is through checking for alerts in the next higher time frame. This will be discussed shortly. The formula for this "heads up" alert is: ((K Range &lt; "X") &lt; OSX) or ((K Range &lt; "X") &gt; OBX) VISUAL ALERT: - text on the screen: CAUTION ROPE IN OVERBOUGHT / OVERSOLD ZONE 3) - ROPE LEAVING OB/OS Zone: When we see a ROPE leaving an extreme zone, this is a potential trading opportunity in itself. We can represent this action using the following formula (BUY example opposite for SELL): C1 ((K Range &lt; "X") &lt; OSX) AND C2 ((K Range &lt; "X") &gt; OSX) VISUAL ALERTS: - Vertical line DARKGREEN (BUY) / CRIMSON (SELL) in the indicator window, thickness of 5 - text on the screen: ROPE BUY / SELL ALERT Note that a crossing of the D% line is not part of the equation, as when a ROPE is formed it is deemed a powerful enough signal to take a trade immediately without waiting for further confirmation. EXIT OPTIONS (BUY example only opposite for SELL) Similar to the trade entry option, there is an amount of evidence we need to gather to make our case for an exit. Lets break them down one at a time. - K AV &lt; D This suggests that the K AV is slowing as it has crossed back beneath the D signal line. This is one piece of evidence that suggests momentum is decreasing, but is not enough to mount a case for trade exit on its own. - D &lt; OBX This indicates that the D signal line is below the Overbought level line. Another piece of evidence, but again, not enough to prove that an exit is viable just yet. - FAST K AV &lt; 50 The FAST K AV data value has crossed beneath the 50 level line. Very often the faster Stoch values will race down below the 50 line, but reverse right back up again, so this is only one piece of evidence, which does not prove your case on its own for an exit. - PRICE &lt; 20 Smoothed MA Price has crossed beneath the 20 Period Smoothed Moving Average. As this MA acts as relative support across all time frames, if price crosses and closes beyond, it is another piece of evidence to suggest that there is weaker momentum. - C1 BBCL &lt; C2 BBCL The current closed candle data value for the Bollinger Band Centre Line is now lower than the value for the previous closed candle. This is a great piece of evidence. Because the BBCL uses a Linear Weighted Moving Average for its calculations, and we all know that Moving Averages are lagging indicators, if a Moving Average suggests price is moving against you, then we almost have an open-shut case. - C1 Smoothed MA &lt; C2 20 Smoothed MA The current closed candle data value for the 20 period Smoothed MA is now lower than the data value for the 20 period Smoothed MA for the previous closed candle. The last piece of evidence we need. BOTH of the Moving Average criteria are included to add a smoothing factor to the trade, as utilising the Stochastic averages themselves can often act as whipsaws and produce false exit signals on their own. VISUAL ALERTS: - Default is a check sign above candle. BUY = RED / SELL = LIME - thickness = 3 TIME TO PROVE YOUR CASE FOR EXIT! So there you go, you have collected your evidence for an exit and have enough to present your case. Just like with the entry criteria, this may seem like a lot of evidence to gather for an exit, and perhaps it is. It may even mean that you lose out on potential profit by staying in a trade slightly too long. But over time the exit criteria work very well and will allow your account to build by keeping you in trades that offer more on the table, without alerting you to exit too early. IMPORTANT NOTE FOR CONSIDERATION IMMEDIATELY AFTER AN EXIT As you exit a trade you may feel there is new evidence appearing for a potential entry, yet no new entry alert appears. This strategy was not designed to be "always in the market". Part of the strategy relies on a condition that we wait for at least one full candle before checking for new entry criteria. Therefore, if you notice that there should be an alert showing on the screen but there isn't, this is the reason. It is important to take a step back after closing a trade and take some time. Hello thread...today I will cover re-entries, as there will be the odd occasion when you have exited and then sometime later price continues on and you see there is potential for more pips. RE-ENTRIES For a re-entry alert to be valid there needs to be a crossing of K AV over the D signal line again, but this may occur anywhere in the indicator window. For ease of use, I have broken this down into 2 possible scenarios. The first covers times when the market is highly volatile and Stochastics are already close to, or inside, an extreme zone. Ordinarily we might be inclined to want to SELL out of an Overbought situation and BUY out of an Oversold, assuming that price momentum is approaching exhaustion when we are at these levels, however it is not uncommon in volatile market movements for price to continue its trajectory for many bars either close to, or beyond these extreme 80/20 levels, criss-crossing above and below the D signal line perhaps many times before a valid exit signal appears. The second scenario covers other instances where Stochastics are outside of the extreme zones, but there is still a cross of the K AV line and the D signal line. Let's look at these separately. 1) RE-ENTRY option 1 – (BUY example only – opposite for SELL trade) One of the regularly recurring behaviours of the multiple Stochastic, and Stochastics in general, is its quirky ability to stay in an extreme zone for many bars whilst the market continues to move in the same direction and not reverse. So how do we recognise this and code the logic so that an alert can suggest this may be happening? (BUY example only - opposite for SELL) C1 (K AV &lt; D) AND C2 ((K AV &gt; D) AND (K AV &gt; OBX)) OK, lets break that down. - C1 (K AV &lt; D) The previous closed candle data value shows that K AV is below the D signal line; - C2 ((K AV &gt; D) AND (K AV &gt; OBX)) The current closed candle data value indicates that K AV has closed back ABOVE the D signal line, and has either moved back into the Overbought zone or was already there. This information suggests a continuation of the bullish trend, and so we can re-enter with a new BUY trade. Then, whilst ever further exit criteria are not met in full, as explained in the EXIT section earlier, we can stay in the BUY and ride it, wave after wave, until there is a definite exit alert presented. VISUAL ALERTS: - Vertical line MAGENTA thickness = 3" - text on screen CAUTION RE-ENTRY IN OVERBOUGHT RE-ENTRY option 2 (BUY example only opposite for SELL trade) Sometimes we will exit a trade, (waiting at least one candle for an opposite directional alert), and conditions return back to indicating movement in the original direction, offering us a chance to re-enter the market with a fresh BUY trade. This generally happens when the K AV group has crossed below the 50 level line, and then crosses back above, and also crosses back above the D signal line. This can occasionally happen fully above the 50 level line, so I have elected to leave all reference to the 50 level line out of the formula, as generally it only requires a cross of K AV below and back above the D signal line. So, the formula: C1 (K AV &lt; D) and C2 ((K AV &gt; D) AND (PRICE &gt; BBCL) AND (C1 BBCL &gt; C2 BBCL)) Lets break that down. 1) C1 (K AV &lt; D) and C2 (K AV &gt; D) If the previous closed candle K AV data value was below the D signal line, and then the current closed candle K AV data value has climbed back ABOVE the D signal line, this is one of the criteria that forms this alert. 2) PRICE &gt; BBCL If PRICE is still above the Bollinger Band Centre Line after C2, this suggests there is still bullish momentum, and forms another criterion to the alert. 3) C2 BBCL &gt; C1 BBCL If the current closed candle shows the Bollinger Band Centre Line data value being greater than that of the previous closed candle, this is the final criterion that forms part of this alert. This indicates that the momentum is still bullish, as the Centre Line (LWMA) values are still increasing. VISUAL ALERTS: - Vertical line MAGENTA thickness = 2 - text on screen CAUTION - RE-ENTRY EXIT FROM RE_ENTRY? Re-entries can be profitable in the extreme, and sometimes they just dont go our way. Thats life! So how do we manage the exit? Quite simply: C2 BBCL &lt; C1 BBCL When the current closed candle data value for the Bollinger Band Centre Line is less than that of the previous candle, this indicates a reversal in momentum, and is a simple enough method of exiting. The reason for this is that while ever the Bollinger Band Centre Line (i.e. 20 period LWMA) is rising, we stay in the trade, as moving averages lag behind price movement. Conversely, the BBCL can only fall if price falls, and we generally only see the BBCL fall when all other exit conditions are already being met. So, for the sake of simplicity in an otherwise complicated world, this is a great way to exit what is a potentially risky entry anyway. VISUAL ALERTS: - Default is a check sign above candle. BUY = RED / SELL = LIME - thickness = 3 Suggestion for changing indicator settings if re-entering. Sometimes these re-entry alerts come at volatile situations and the move can produce a nice windfall. Using standard settings of, say, 12/3/3 won't produce the same signals as the new indicator will offer you, as the input value for K% is coming from the averaging of 19 individual K% lines. And of course because the D% line is a derivative of K%, the D% line will look different on your computer screen using my indicator than if you were using the standard Stoch settings. So I am using a "static" value of "12" for the D% signal line, which of course is derived from a 12 period moving average of "K AV" ( not a specific K% value). I am using a higher input value for D% as it offers a smoothing technique to the input signal for an alert. A D% value of "8" works nicely also, but sticking to "3" is not reliable for a decent signal IMHO. I encourage people to play around with their own settings of course. For the initial set-up alert however, (taking a BUY alert as the example) it is the Average of SLOW K (K11 to K19) that needs to cross above the D% signal line to produce this first alert. The reason being that K AV itself may skew the logic, especially when FAST K (the average of K1 to K10) may have already traveled into the overbought zone while SLOW K is still in oversold. If SLOW K has risen above D then we have more evidence of a possible change in momentum. FAST K may well return back to oversold within a few bars otherwise. See the screen-shot detailing 2 examples in close proximity where FAST K AV has broken away and yet SLOW K AV has lagged behind. Price continued down about 22 pips across this period on the M1 chart. Unless SLOW K AV is rising above D% we have no indication of a change in momentum. Using a standard Stochastic, whether it was 21/12/3 or 6/3/3, we would have seen a crossover of K% and D% and thought we were getting a signal to buy, which of course would have been false. Having only two Stochastic lines, K and D, is not enough to tell the whole story, whereas an averaging of all 19, with the addition of the FAST and the SLOW averaged groups offers us so much more information, enabling us to more clearly see changes in momentum over time. OK, sorry for delay...have had a friend staying here with my wife and I and we decided to take off up north for a night. Back now, so here we go! MTF - Multiple Time Frame analysis. The "meat and potatoes" of this strategy is really about locating the best entries; the ones that produce the most pips with the least draw-down / risk. Whilst an entry on a 1 Minute chart might LOOK like a spectacular idea at the time, a quick check of what is happening on the 5 minute and higher can often reveal a completely different story, and what you might have hoped for may turn out to be a silly mistake that just cost you valuable PIPs! It's not always enough to have "ALL THE EVIDENCE" from a single time frame. The bigger picture is revealed when you zoom out and notice that your chosen time frame is just a small part of something much larger. What may seem like a decent upward trend forming on a 1 minute chart may simply be a bearish retracement on a 15 minute chart, and so on. And so the evidence we think we have ready to present our "case for entry" may quickly become tainted if we don't consider the bigger picture, and check for confluent entry conditions on higher time frames. So how should we do this? The indicator is responsible for doing a lot of heavy lifting so we don't have to, and has been designed in such a way that certain alerts can be switched on, and others off. EXAMPLE - A QUICK SCALP of the 1 MINUTE TF with confluence from 5 MINUTE TF The time is 05:40 hrs, and you have a near-complete set of setup alerts and things are looking good for a BUY on the 1 minute time frame. You are waiting for the all-important 4th alert, which says you can pull the trigger... If you have a 5 minute chart open, with the same indicator on it, but this time you change the settings so that only the first few setup alerts are switched on, then it is far easier to know when there are confluent conditions present in the market. Why not have the indicator on the 5 minute AND the 15 minute, for FURTHER confirmation and confidence in your entry? See the following series of screen-shots to illustrate this example. Screen-shot 1 shows a 5 minute time frame chart of the AUDUSD on 29 Nov. Notice how there is setup alerts 1 (WHITE vertical line) and 2 (GOLD vertical line)? In screen-shot 2 we see similar alerts starting to show on the 1 minute chart. And by the time we get a decent entry signal on the 1 minute, there has been at least 2 setup alerts on the 5 minute as confirmation that we are getting in at a decent time. Regarding stop loss, I have just placed it beyond the recent low for this example. Take Profit is the Daily Open above us. Notice on the 1 minute how it appears there may have been an earlier entry alert? It looks like K AV may have risen above 50, however in actual fact, one of the pieces of evidence for entry was missing, i.e. FAST K AV had already crossed above the 80 overbought level. So the actual entry came about 10 minutes later where the green vertical line is showing...entry should be at the open of next candle. This was a great entry, with no draw-down. 15 pips banked (minus spread)...it may have taken a while (around 45 minutes) but a win is a win, and I'll take it! You will no doubt see that there was an obvious exit signal showing mid-way through the trade, yes? It is pretty clear that K AV MUST have crossed below the 50 line, but again, there is evidence missing from our exit criteria, namely the BBCL is still rising. It actually starts to fall at 06:16 hrs, just prior to when the market starts its' move up. BUT! Even if the BBCL started to fall PRIOR to this, I would stay in the trade, because we are looking at Multiple Time Frames not just for entry, but in trade management also. If I was only trading the 1 minute without checking the bigger picture, I would have received an exit alert at 06:16 hrs (JUST before the market picked up!), but by keeping an eye on the 5 minute chart, where we can see that momentum is still bullish, we keep the faith. It is always easy to back-test these strategies and find ideal scenarios that worked perfectly, and it is not always that straight-forward in real-time, but for illustration purposes this example shows how following the alerts and being a little bit patient can win out and give you the return you are after.

To clarify the K/D crossover SV value thing... When a line appears, it means a TF2 crossover of K and D has occurred. It is here that we need to check the separation value of K and D. If we are using the 5 Minute chart as TF2 and the 1 Minute as TF1, this means that there are 5 "minutes" of data along each flat section of the TF2 stoch K line. If you position your mouse over the open of the TF2 Stoch and check the value, and then move your mouse along the stoch slowly, candle by candle, you will see the data values in the window change slightly with each candle. This is not easy to fix, as Stochastics by their very nature use either high/low or close/close for their calculations. There would need to be a Stochastic that used the OPEN in order for an open price to be printed and effectively "frozen", perfectly flat, for the 5 minute duration. Such a Stoch does not exist, and MT4 would not recognise "open" as the input price field if it were to be programmed into the code. Therefore it is always best practice to position your mouse over the very last bar at the very end of the TF2 K line, to get the values at candle close. This is the best bet you have in seeing the most accurate values to base your decision on whether to enter or not.

Unfortunately you cannot have it both ways, very early in a trend, and no signal if it's not going to be the start of a trend. That would be akin to magic. All we need is a system that gets a big chunk of the trending move but doesn't lose too much in a range. There will be losses no matter what you do. I did a manual backtest using the original rules found in the manual - 6 months, 2 pairs. Of course, 6 months is very little but the results are good. The key is to refrain from adding new rules or indicators when you have several consecutive losses (it did happen and it will happen again). If you come up with rules that keep you out of those whipsaws you also lose many of the nice early entries to follow a trend. If on the other hand you want to catch the big moves even sooner you get killed by whipsaws. I'm not against changing rules or adding new indicators, but any such change should come only after some serious backtesting to confirm that overall, over hundreds of trades, it makes the system more profitable. What happens instead is that as soon as people notice a few losses they try to filter them out, unaware of the fact that their filter leads to more problems than it solves.

--- 帖子 #91 ---
Your MA-cross look very good. For the whipsaws we could create a locking - unlocking method. What is that ? Lock = no SL but at the SL where SL would sit, we place a pending order in opposite direction of the active order with same lot-size . If market triggers that level, we have 2 active orders with same lot-size. One is long, the other is short. From there market can go where it wants to go. If it goes up, somewhere it will reverse and form a new Short-entry. At that level we could close the Long order and go Short ---&gt; we would have 2 active Shorts then. Now we only need a certain move downwards to break even and there we can close both Short trades. In other words: Whenever price would hit SL, we will use the lock-unlock method to achieve a Breakeven . Just an idea.

I use this strategy also but I use it for scalping and not long term trading. One thing to note that has helped me avoid losing trades is to look at the ADR. ( I know it has been mentioned in this thread somewhere so I am just reiterating ) If the ADR has already been reached exercise extreme caution if if all the signals line up . For example this morning there was a valid signal given to me on the EUR/USD all the rules had been met however upon looking at the ADR I noticed it had already been reached and was ahead 40 pips. When checking where the SL would be if I decided to take this trade I saw it would have been 45 pips away. Now to expect the EUR to move ANOTHER 45 pips from where it is now ( assuming a 1:1 R/R ) after an already huge move is unlikely. Could it happen? Of course but as traders we need to look at odds and probabilities. After a few candles the pair did in fact reverse thus keeping me from a losing trade. My point is even if all the rules are met make sure to check the basic stuff also , do not just pull the trigger blindly. In this case you can see that the research would have paid off and kept you out of a bad trade.

Developed under the guidance of a mentor, it is tailored for scalping, relying on favorable market conditions such as dynamic movement and minimal spreads . Trading System Rules: Timeframes: Trade on lower timeframes like M1-M5. EMA Condition: Price must be above the 20 EMA for a buy and below for a sell. Candle Condition: Candle color must change. Setup Bar Criteria: Setup bar must be a strong bar, closing within 75% of its range. Volume of the setup bar must be higher than the previous bar. ATR Requirement: ATR(14) (Average True Range) must be above 4 pips, or inside NY KZ ( this occurs from 8:00 AM to 12:00 PM (EST), when both the London and New York sessions are active. This period is known for increased trading volume and volatility in the forex market) Targets and Stops: Target: Aim for 1R (one times the range of the setup candle). Stop Loss: Can be placed very close to the setup candle's low (for buys) or high (for sells). Working Theories: The system should be traded manually. A major trend line or support and resistance break can create very good opportunities for the system which we can refer to as (Bullish / Bearish Breakout Phase) Trade cautiously if the setup candle is very large. If the entire candle range including the the wicks from top to bottom is 100%, then the close of the candle shouldn’t be lower than 75% of the total range. Measure the candle and mark the 75% level and make sure the close is beyond that. Same thing for a short. If the total pips of the red bar it’s 10, than 7.5 of those pips should be above the close, meaning the close is towards the bottom of the entire range of the candle.

Ignore this if you please it's completely optional and not necessary to make money, but could be a worthy addition to the methodology. There's another advanced mechanical method to determining HTF directional bias. It's a method that's used in another powerful system, but this scalping system can prosper from it. I'll mention it for food for thought. There's a concept called Follow Through. Using the H4 as the anchor, we observe price as it approaches a powerful level. Personally I prefer Pivot Points. The area between the last two pivot points is considered the dealing range. If price moves beyond it and hasn't printed a new pivot, just extend it. Once a range is set, the top half of the range is considered "Premium" and the Bottom half of the range is considered "Discount" You can only take short positions when price is in "Premium". You can only take long positions when price is in "Discount" If two bars close beyond the Pivot Point level we consider this a Strong Follow Through. If one bar closes beyond the Pivot Point level and the next bar closes not beyond it, we consider this a Weak Follow Through If price wicks beyond the Pivot Point level and the next bar closes not beyond the level this is considered a no follow through. If price makes a No Follow Through to the downside, then we have Bullish Bias. If price makes a No Follow Through to the upside , then we have Bearish Bias. If price makes a Strong Follow Through to the upside, then we have Bullish Bias. If price makes a Strong Follow Through to the downside, then we have a Bearish Bias. If price makes a Weak Follow Through, trade cautiously Once the dealing range and the premium/discount has been setup, on your entry timeframe you wait for price to sweep liquidity ideally inside a Killzone. With this additional method you can target 1:2 RR and use more traditional SL's and TPs. Once liquidity has been swept you can now start to look for scalping setups with the original setup. working concept: ignore I’m observing a more fined turned approach to the volume increase rule. Theres advanced indicators out there that can fulfill the same purpose of that rule but with more of a nuanced analysis. Larger volume can often tell us multiple things, but most importantly it tells us that there’s liquidity in the play at hand and confirms our alignment with larger players. However, well this is important it may be redundant with ATR and Percent candle close rule. So using the same logic but with a different approach I’m looking at the relationship between price movement and volume. Instead of just looking if the volume candle is larger, we look to see what volume is telling us in relation to the way price moved.For example, If price made a major move to the downside relative to the ATR but the volume was significantly lower than previous bars, this can imply that the bears have control of the market because the bulls are not there. So the volume may be lower but it can actually imply the opposite of what we presumed this far. The TradingView indicator I’m using to measure this effect is called EVEREX. The more the bar is filled up, the more effort it took to move price. people who have followed me know I am a fan of correlating volume with price movement. Let’s take a look at this example. This is a pullback after a major impulsive move to the downside broke structure.After price pulled back above the Ema it made a lovely 75 candle below the Ema within ATR on a color change, but the problem is we read the volume as insufficient because it’s decreased. But what EVEREX does is put context to that. Yes there may be less volume but that’s because there’s less bull too. As you can see price required well below the average effort to propel the candle lower. This should actually be a sign of weakness by the bulls and an opportunity for the system. it’s a working concept

Since I thought this might help traders on both threads I'll post it here too: "So as I'm looking over asdf's charts examining Renko price action along with the commentary (thank you by the way, they are most helpful) I was thinking about a couple of patterns and why they are relevant to future price action. Here is something you might think about if you have not already. Since renko is not time sensitive like other methods of charting then head and shoulder patterns really have a different significance. Or possibly the same significance but for different reasons. It is likely that speculative traders who use renko are in the minority. Therefore most traders do not see the same renko patterns and because of this, do not make trading decisions based off of said patterns. Imagine this example: Using a 10 tic brick, one head and shoulders pattern could take 5 hours to form using renko. That same pattern could then repeat itself in 1 hour... or 30 minutes... The point is that realtime price action might not resemble a head and two shoulders at all. What IS significant is that despite the time it takes to form the renko pattern, support and resistance levels form. Historical S/R levels are not affected by time passing, but rather by recency bias. That is, the impact a particular price point, level, zone (whatever you want to call it) has on the collective trader's psyche. My point is that unless renko traders start to become very common, it might be better to attribute a winning expectancy using a renko pattern to S/R rather than the pattern itself. What do you all think?"

This example is for an up move reversing to a down move: As I watch the 1st reversal brick forming I place a Pending Sell Stop Order at the projected brick Close price with a 10 pip + spread SL. Last piece of indicator advice. The preference would be to lose them altogether, because then it is easy to follow price and only price without distractions--which is a great way to learn for those just starting out. It is evident, though, that many still want to use them. I hope this will add some perspective. If your plan is to get to the point where you can trade for a living, then your charts will literally become the tools of your profession. With that in mind, the faster you become proficient at using your tools, the faster you can trade for a living. Continually tweaking, changing, switching, altering, replacing your charts, indicators, timeframes, trading methods, etc. postpones proficiency. Each change of a chart requires a new adjustment period before you become proficient at using the new change. The more changes, the more adjustment periods. Simple as that. For anyone who is proficient in a sport that uses "tools," such as golf, tennis, hockey, archery, shooting, etc., knows the importance of picking a "tool" and then sticking with it and learning how to use it. Same goes with occupations such as surgeons, dentists, etc. Imagine Tiger Woods showing up at a tournament with a new set of clubs, week after week after week. Or Roger Federer switching racquets. Or going into brain surgery only to have the surgeon tell you, "I've been working on a few tweaks to this procedure!" In the end, it is not the indicator that will make a trader successful, it is the trader's ability to use the indicator that makes her successful. But that success will not come overnight, no matter what kind of indicator it is. And that success will definitely not come if a trader is looking at new indicator after new indicator after new indicator, with one setting one week and another setting another week. And therein lies the rub. Most new traders will search and tweak and switch and change for hours upon hours, looking for the "perfect" chart setup, thinking it is the chart that makes the money rather then the trader's ability to use the chart that makes the money. In all actuality, every change of the chart and every new indicator could have been used to make money, as long as the trader would have taken the time to become proficient in its use. So why not pick a set of tools, establish them as "your tools," eliminate the option of changing or tweaking, and instead, spend the hours upon hours learning how to become proficient in using them. That's what's required for success. Unfortunately, that's not what most new traders want to hear. Hope that helps.

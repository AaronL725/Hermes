OK, let me start by saying that this is a purely mechanical system. There are many versions which I will later post but this one is the core concept. It is based on the principle that the market has to move, so we are counting on volatility to help us out. No indicators, no continuous monitoring needed. Oanda is your best choice if you plan to try this out. They have a good spread, you can trade directly from the charts, and your positions can be as small as your money management can afford. Oanda cancels a buy and a sell on the same currency pair (hedge) so you need to open a sub-account, and divide your money equally among them. So if you have a $10,000 account you would have $5,000 on the primary and $5,000 on the sub-account. For this system I only trade the EURUSD since it has the lowest spreads , which is very important. Anytime between 22:00GMT or 0:00GMT place your orders. Let's say your primary account will manage the long trades and your sub-account the short ones. Primary account.- Place your limit buy order on the next multiple of 20 and 4 more to follow 20 pips apart from the previous one. NO stop loss, NO take profit. e.g. if the price is 1.4572 you would enter 5 orders to buy at the following prices, 1.4580, 1.4600, 1.4620, 1.4640, and 1.4660. Sub-account.- Do the same as above but to the short side with sell orders. e.g. 1.4560, 1.4540, 1.4520, 1.4500, and 1.4480. Money management.- VERY important, each of your orders has to be exactly for the same number of units and it should be allocated as follows: 300 units per $100 dollars on the account, that is a 3 cent pip. So in our example, our account after splitting it is $5,000 so we will buy and sell 15000 units per order. Wait for a big move. You will have several scenarios when you come back next day at the same time. Maybe, only one side of your orders were triggered and you only have positive pips. Maybe one order on one of the sides was triggered and two on the other side. You will most likely have positive and negative pips or only negative pips. But only close all your positions when in profit. e.g. you have a 100 positive pips on the long side but a 50 negative pips on the short side, you should close all for a profit of 50. If the market went 100 pips up and 100 pips down and then came back to 1.4570ish, you will have 200 negative pips (hence the Money Management) DO NOT CLOSE your positions until you are in profit. When and if you have taken your profit, repeat the process. Important.- sometimes you need to take a loss, especially when you have open positions before big news such as NFP, I prefer to close my positions on the weekend as well due to excessive widening of the spread. I know you will have many questions and I can almost bet that I am missing something that I don't remember now but that's why we are all here to help each other. So don't be shy and ask away. Also if you see any room for improvement I'd be more than happy to hear it. Hope you all like it!! Will be posting the advanced versions later.

The good thing about grid systems is that they are easy to back test with 90% accuracy since there are no indicators involved (usually). The bad thing about traditional (non-dynamic) grids is that for every one position you open against the trend (whatever it ends up being), you need at least 2.5 trades open in the direction of the trend in order to break even that 1 position you opened against the trend. Example: EURUSD starts at 1.5800 and eventually goes to 1.5900 1st entry: Short 1.5800 2nd entry: Long 1.5820 3rd entry: Short 1.5780 4th entry: Long 1.5840 5th entry: Long 1.5860 6th entry: Long 1.5880 7th entry: Long 1.5900 If the price stayed around 1.5900, you would be in the negative, but close to break even. If there is a retracement to say around 1.5870 or something, than those1.5880 and 1.5900 so you are again in the negative... In my opinion the only grid trading system that may work is carry trade gridding on one side; long positive swap. With proper money management it may work. Hedge funds, investment banks, companies etc do carry trades to hedge other investments. OK traders, now that you have gotten the hang of it, I shall elaborate on other techniques to maximize profits and minimize risk. The system is based on several methods that I have picked up along the way, which were good but did not convince me fully. THE STRADDLE - First of all, use a straddle so as to catch any possible movement of the market be it to which ever side it goes to. This is a profitable system that with the use of fibonacci levels you have to predict a TP and a SL. The pros is that it has a good winning record, the cons is that you leave lots of profit on the table or even when in profit the market turns against you and your positions is stopped out at SL. Also, this is a very popular method and traders calculate entries up to the pip so it's a lot easier for brokers to hunt SL and your target could be missed for as little as a pip or even less. THE GRID - Basic grids work by setting buy and sell orders at intervals and counting on the market to return to the median. The pros is that you catch and profit every time there is movement. The cons is that your loss can grow as much as your profit hence you have to be very careful with money management, this reduces your value per pip dramatically since you have to be prepared to run a 3000-5000 drawdown depending on the pair traded. THE HEDGE - There are many techniques for hedging, basically to control losses and buy time to decide on how to manage a losing position. Others tend to take advantage of the interest rate such as the long EURUSD/USDCHF hedge. There is also the impeccable hedge where you would go long on the EURUSD and USD CHF and short on the EURCHF and try to profit from any correction in the market, some people even think this is impossible. The pros are that you collect positive interest if you hold on to the position long enough to make it worthwhile and that when the market trends you get a handsome profit. The cons are that money management is crucial to withstand corrections in the market and again you have to reduce your lots in order to avoid a margin call. So you need a big account to make ends meet. Basically my system, tries to get the best of these three systems with some important points in mind: No indicators. Indicators calculate price movements and average prices in order to output "the signal" so most of them are lagging. Your best indicator is your price chart. Indicators also tend to be subject to the discretion of the trader, for some breaching a resistance level is a signal of a buy for others is a signal of retracement, some even say that it can either go violently up or down (duuh!! thanks for the tip). Being in an overbought market doesn't necessarily mean that the move is over. So well first point is no indicators. No prediction. It is very stressing for some people to predict whether the market will go up, down or sideways. If you think about it the chances are like 33% for each, so you need an edge. My edge is not to predict market movements and go wherever the flow (market) takes me, so I will not close positions on a stagnated price, so just by this I am trying to increase my chances to 50% and then by straddling I am sure I will get in the market. No stress - Very important when trading. Yes, I've read "trading in the zone" and even tried hypnosis (kidding to eliminate the stress from trading. Until I realized that the easiest way to eliminate the stress is by trading small positions. The problem of trading small positions is that your return is small as well and this in turn stresses you. So the idea of adding to winning positions increases your return exponentially and your hedge is there to protect you from any unforeseen events. I'd take a 7.8% loss against a margin call any day of the week. And as you can see from backtest it is not that common. The system, though extremely simple, works very well. Returns on Investment (ROI) go anywhere from 10% to 30% depending on the market conditions. But we always want more, am I right?? well here's more: Advance Techniques If you are able to babysit your trades or monitor them on an hourly basis well then this is a technique for you. When in profit, be it on the short side or the long side, close the losing positions and set a stoploss to the winning positions at the breakeven of the last winning position. And place other set of orders on both sides. Max 5 trades per side again. By doing this you are locking in profit and adding to a winning position but still controlling your risk. This backtest like any other is not 100% accurate, since we do not know many factors that were prevalent at the moment, so it's just an estimate of what could have happened during this period if a certain system had been traded, with an 80 to 95% accuracy. But, yes. If you would like to increase accuracy you would have to check all possible dates in which the spread widened and due to this factor you did not enter trade orders. But then also have an analysis of the trades that you would have gotten out from and the ones that you would have stayed till profit was met as the system requires it. You would have to analyze each day separately. It is a good point though, and I will try to include in our forward testing the exits and advanced techniques at play as much in real time as possible.
